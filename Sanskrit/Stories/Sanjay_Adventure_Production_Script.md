# संजयस्य साहसम् · Sanjay's Adventure — Production Script

A guide for generating illustrations in Google Flow (or any AI image/video tool) and recording a voiceover, for the 10-page Sanskrit graphic novel.

---

## 1. Character & Style Bible

Paste this once at the start of your Flow session (or reuse as a "style anchor" in every prompt) so the characters stay consistent across all 10 images.

**Art style:** warm, flat-color children's picture-book illustration, hand-painted textured look, soft rounded shapes, gentle gouache/watercolor texture, gentle daylight, similar to modern Indian children's book illustration (think Tulika Books / Karadi Tales style). No photorealism, no 3D render look, no text or letters anywhere in the image.

**Sanjay (main character):**
A cheerful Indian boy, about 6 years old, warm brown skin, short black hair, big curious dark eyes, wearing a bright pink/magenta kurta and dark blue-purple shorts, barefoot or in simple sandals. Expressive, animated poses — never static. Consistent across every image.

**Nalini (Sanjay's mother):**
An Indian woman in her early 30s, warm brown skin, long dark hair tied back, wearing a golden-yellow saree, gentle kind expression. Appears only on page 10 (and briefly implied on page 1).

**Setting progression:** village home → dusty village path → open green field → farmyard → forest edge → deep forest → dusk forest → nightfall path back to village home. Keep a consistent warm color palette: soft blues and yellows for daytime, deepening to purples and indigo for evening/night pages.

**Consistency tip for Flow:** if the tool supports reference images or character locking, generate Sanjay once on page 1, then use that same generated image as a style/character reference for every subsequent prompt so his face, outfit, and proportions don't drift.

## 2. Page-by-Page Image Prompts

Each prompt is written to be pasted directly into Google Flow. Aspect ratio suggestion: 16:9 (landscape), since that matches typical video/slideshow use.

### Page 1 — At Home (गृहे)
> A cheerful 6-year-old Indian boy named Sanjay, warm brown skin, short black hair, big curious eyes, wearing a bright pink kurta and blue-purple shorts, standing restlessly outside a small colorful village house with a red triangular roof, blue-shuttered windows, and a thatched look, under a bright daytime sky with a few soft clouds and green grass yard. He looks eager and a little bored, hands on hips, about to step outside. Warm, flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, soft daylight, gentle textures, no text in image.

### Page 2 — The Dog (श्वानः)
> The same boy Sanjay, in pink kurta and blue shorts, walking happily along a dusty village path lined with a few small trees, waving at a friendly brown-and-white dog with floppy ears standing at a house doorway, tail wagging mid-motion. Bright daytime sky, warm earthy tones, flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, joyful expressions, no text in image.

### Page 3 — The Cow (गौः)
> Sanjay in his pink kurta and blue shorts standing at the edge of a green open field, looking up admiringly at a large gentle white cow with black and brown patches grazing on grass, soft morning sun in the corner of the sky. Warm pastel green and blue palette, flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, calm peaceful mood, no text in image.

### Page 4 — Goat and Hen (अजा कुक्कुटी च)
> Sanjay in his pink kurta walking through a small farmyard, with a white-and-tan goat with small horns standing nearby and a red-and-orange hen with a comb pecking at the ground close by, green grass and a low wooden fence in the background, bright cheerful daylight. Flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, playful farmyard scene, no text in image.

### Page 5 — The Peacock (मयूरः)
> Sanjay, small and delighted, entering the edge of a lush green forest with tall trees, watching a magnificent peacock with a fully fanned-out blue-green tail covered in eye-spot patterns, mid-dance, feathers glowing in dappled forest sunlight. Rich jewel-toned blues, greens, and teals contrasted with Sanjay's pink kurta, flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, sense of wonder and awe, no text in image.

### Page 6 — The Monkey (वानरः)
> Sanjay standing beneath a tall forest tree, laughing and reaching up toward a playful brown monkey swinging from a branch above him, tossing a small fruit between them, dense green forest canopy filtering soft sunlight. Warm brown and green palette, flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, fun energetic motion, no text in image.

### Page 7 — The Elephant (गजः)
> Sanjay standing small and a little awestruck at the base of a huge gray elephant with big flapping ears and a raised curling trunk, deep in a dense green forest with tall trees, dappled afternoon light. Emphasize scale — the elephant should tower over Sanjay. Flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, gentle sense of amazement mixed with mild fear, no text in image.

### Page 8 — Deer and Parrot (हरिणः शुकः च)
> Sanjay walking quietly through a forest clearing, watching a graceful spotted brown deer with small antlers eating grass, while a bright green parrot with a red beak perches on a nearby branch calling out. The sky in the background is just beginning to turn a warm orange-pink at the edges, hinting at evening. Flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, soft golden-hour light starting to creep in, no text in image.

### Page 9 — Lost at Evening (सायंकाले)
> Sanjay standing alone and a little scared in a darkening forest at dusk, deep purple and indigo sky with a pale rising moon, silhouettes of tall trees around him, looking around in every direction searching for the way home, small twinkling stars appearing. Flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, moody but not frightening, gentle purple-blue palette, emphasize his small vulnerable posture, no text in image.

### Page 10 — Home Again (माता आगच्छति)
> A warm nighttime reunion scene: Sanjay running happily into the arms of his mother Nalini, who wears a golden-yellow saree and holds a glowing oil lamp, both smiling, standing on the path just outside their cozy lit village home with warm yellow light glowing from the windows, deep blue night sky with stars and a crescent moon above. Flat-color children's picture-book illustration style, warm golden lamp-light contrasted against the cool blue night, heartwarming and tender mood, no text in image.

## 3. Voiceover Script (with timing cues)

Two voiceover versions are provided below: **Sanskrit + English** (original) and **Sanskrit + Telugu** (కొత్తది). Use whichever matches your audience, or record both as separate audio tracks.

Read each line as: **Sanskrit (slow, clear) → pause → translation (natural pace)**. Suggested pace: slow enough for a child learner to follow along with the page. Approx. 25–30 seconds per page including pauses works well for narration syncing with images/video.

Recommended tools: ElevenLabs, Google's Text-to-Speech (Chirp/WaveNet — choose a Telugu voice for the Telugu track, an Indian-English voice for the English track), Murf.ai, or Descript — all support importing this script directly. If recording your own voice, read Sanskrit slowly and phonetically using the transliteration as your pronunciation guide.

### 3a. Sanskrit + English Version

**Page 1 — At Home**
- adya sañjayaḥ gṛhe asti. *(pause)* Today Sanjay is at home.
- sañjayaḥ bālaḥ cañcalaḥ. *(pause)* Sanjay is a restless boy.
- kiṃ sañjayaḥ sukhī? *(pause)* Is Sanjay happy?
- sañjayaḥ na sukhī. *(pause)* Sanjay is not happy.
- sañjayaḥ bahiḥ gantum icchati. *(pause)* Sanjay wants to go outside.
- "ahaṃ grāmaṃ vanaṃ ca gacchāmi!" *(pause, excited tone)* "I am going to the village and the forest!"

**Page 2 — The Dog**
- sañjayaḥ mārge gacchati. *(pause)* Sanjay walks along the path.
- "bhau! bhau!" *(bark sound effect or playful tone)* "Woof! Woof!"
- ekaḥ śvānaḥ gṛhadvāre tiṣṭhati. *(pause)* A dog stands at a house-door.
- śvānaḥ pālitaḥ paśuḥ. *(pause)* The dog is a pet (raised) animal.
- "namaste, śvāna!" *(cheerful)* "Hello, dog!"
- śvānaḥ pucchaṃ cālayati. *(pause)* The dog wags its tail.

**Page 3 — The Cow**
- "ambhā!" *(moo sound effect or deep tone)* "Moo!"
- sañjayaḥ kṣetraṃ paśyati. *(pause)* Sanjay looks at a field.
- tatra gauḥ tṛṇaṃ khādati. *(pause)* There a cow eats grass.
- gauḥ api pālitaḥ paśuḥ. *(pause)* The cow is also a pet/raised animal.
- gauḥ dugdhaṃ dadāti. *(pause)* The cow gives milk.
- "dhanyavādaḥ, go-mātā!" *(warm tone)* "Thank you, mother cow!"

**Page 4 — Goat and Hen**
- pathi ajā api asti. *(pause)* There is a goat on the path too.
- "mem mem!" *(bleat sound effect)* "Baa baa!"
- tatra kukkuṭī api carati. *(pause)* A hen also wanders there.
- "kakaka kakaka!" *(cluck sound effect)* "Cluck cluck!"
- śvānaḥ, gauḥ, ajā, kukkuṭī — sarve pālitāḥ paśavaḥ. *(slightly slower, listing tone)* Dog, cow, goat, hen — all are pet/domestic animals.
- idānīṃ sañjayaḥ vanaṃ prati gacchati. *(pause)* Now Sanjay goes towards the forest.

**Page 5 — The Peacock**
- "kekā! kekā!" *(bird-call tone)* "Kay-ka! Kay-ka!" (peacock's call)
- sañjayaḥ vanaṃ praviśati. *(pause)* Sanjay enters the forest.
- tatra sundaraḥ mayūraḥ nṛtyati. *(pause, wondrous tone)* There a beautiful peacock is dancing.
- mayūraḥ vanyaḥ paśuḥ. *(pause)* The peacock is a wild animal.
- "aho! kīdṛśāḥ picchāḥ!" *(delighted)* "Oh! What wonderful feathers!"
- sañjayaḥ hasati, ānandaṃ labhate. *(pause)* Sanjay laughs, and feels joy.

**Page 6 — The Monkey**
- "hā! hā! hā!" *(playful laughter)* "Ha! Ha! Ha!" — laughter from above.
- ekaḥ vānaraḥ śākhāyāṃ krīḍati. *(pause)* A monkey plays on a branch.
- vānaraḥ api vanyaḥ paśuḥ. *(pause)* The monkey too is a wild animal.
- "mayā saha krīḍa!" *(inviting tone)* "Play with me!" (says the monkey)
- sañjayaḥ vānareṇa saha krīḍati. *(pause)* Sanjay plays with the monkey.
- dvau api phalāni kṣipataḥ. *(pause)* Both of them toss fruits.

**Page 7 — The Elephant**
- "bṛṃ! bṛṃ!" *(low trumpeting tone)* "Brrm! Brrm!" — a trumpeting sound.
- mahān gajaḥ vṛkṣān cālayati. *(pause, slower/deeper)* A big elephant shakes the trees.
- gajaḥ atīva bṛhat paśuḥ. *(pause)* The elephant is a very huge animal.
- gajaḥ api vanyaḥ paśuḥ. *(pause)* The elephant is also a wild animal.
- sañjayaḥ kiñcit bhītaḥ, kiñcit vismitaḥ. *(pause)* Sanjay is a little afraid, a little amazed.
- "tvaṃ śāntaḥ asi, gaja!" *(soft, reassured)* "You are calm, elephant!"

**Page 8 — Deer and Parrot**
- ekaḥ sundaraḥ hariṇaḥ tṛṇaṃ khādati. *(pause)* A beautiful deer eats grass.
- hariṇaḥ śīghraṃ dhāvati. *(pause, quicker pace)* The deer runs swiftly.
- "rāma! rāma!" *(bird-call tone)* "Rama! Rama!" (the parrot's call)
- vṛkṣe śukaḥ śabdaṃ karoti. *(pause)* A parrot on the tree makes a sound.
- hariṇaḥ śukaḥ ca — ubhau vanyau paśū. *(pause)* The deer and the parrot — both are wild animals.
- ākāśaḥ kiñcit raktavarṇaḥ bhavati. *(pause, tone shifting quieter)* The sky begins to turn a little red.

**Page 9 — Lost at Evening**
- sūryaḥ astaṃ gacchati. ākāśaḥ raktavarṇaḥ. *(slower, softer)* The sun is setting. The sky is red.
- sañjayaḥ caturdiśaṃ paśyati. *(pause)* Sanjay looks in every direction.
- gajaḥ, hariṇaḥ, śukaḥ — ke api na santi. *(pause)* The elephant, deer, parrot — none of them are there.
- gṛhaṃ kutra asti? *(uncertain tone)* Where is home?
- "ahaṃ mārgaṃ na jānāmi..." *(worried, trailing off)* "I don't know the way..."
- sañjayaḥ kiñcit bhītaḥ bhavati. *(pause)* Sanjay becomes a little afraid.

**Page 10 — Home Again**
- "sañjaya! sañjaya!" *(calling from a distance, warm)* "Sanjay! Sanjay!" — a voice calls from afar.
- nalinī, sañjayasya mātā, dīpaṃ dhārayati. *(pause)* Nalini, Sanjay's mother, holds a lamp.
- "mātaḥ! aham atra!" *(relieved, joyful)* "Mother! I am here!"
- nalinī sañjayaṃ paśyati. sukhinī bhavati. *(pause, warm)* Nalini sees Sanjay and becomes happy.
- "ahaṃ śvānaṃ, gāṃ, ajāṃ, kukkuṭīṃ, mayūraṃ, vānaraṃ, gajaṃ, hariṇaṃ, śukaṃ ca apaśyam!" *(excited, faster)* "I saw a dog, cow, goat, hen, peacock, monkey, elephant, deer, and parrot!"
- "śobhanam, putra! adhunā gṛhaṃ gacchāvaḥ." *(gentle, closing tone)* "Wonderful, son! Now let's go home."

## 4. Practical Tips

- **Consistency across Flow generations:** if Flow lets you re-use a generated character image as a reference for later prompts, always feed page 1's Sanjay back in — this is the single biggest lever for keeping him recognizable page to page.
- **Batch by mood, not by page order:** generate all "bright daytime" pages (1–6) in one session, then switch to "dusk/night" pages (9–10) in another, so the tool's internal lighting logic stays consistent within each batch.
- **Voiceover pacing:** record page-by-page as separate audio clips (10 files) rather than one long file — this makes it trivial to sync each clip to its matching illustration later in any video editor.
- **Sound effects:** for the animal-call lines (क्वा, भौ, अम्भा, में, ककक, केका, हा, बृं, राम) consider layering a real/royalty-free animal sound effect under the voice line for extra engagement.
- **Choosing a Telugu TTS voice:** Google Cloud TTS and Murf.ai both offer native Telugu voices (look for locale `te-IN`) that will read the Telugu script naturally — no need to romanize the Telugu text. Keep the Sanskrit lines in a separate voice/track if you want a clear language switch cue for the child listening.
