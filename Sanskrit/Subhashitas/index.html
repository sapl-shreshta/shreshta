१९
केयूराणि न भूषयन्ति... Speech alone is a person's true ornament 💬
▼
📖 Sanskrit Text & Transliteration
केयूराणि न भूषयन्ति पुरुषं हारा न चन्द्रोज्ज्वला न स्नानं न विलेपनं न कुसुमं नालङ्कृता मूर्धजाः । वाण्येका समलङ्करोति पुरुषं या संस्कृता धार्यते क्षीयन्ते खलु भूषणानि सततं वाग्भूषणं भूषणम् ॥ १९॥
keyūrāṇi na bhūṣayanti puruṣaṃ hārā na candrojjvalā na snānaṃ na vilepanaṃ na kusumaṃ nālaṅkṛtā mūrdhajāḥ | vāṇyekā samalaṅkaroti puruṣaṃ yā saṃskṛtā dhāryate kṣīyante khalu bhūṣaṇāni satataṃ vāgbhūṣaṇaṃ bhūṣaṇam || 19||

💡 Overall Meaning
Armlets do not truly adorn a person, nor do necklaces bright as the moon, nor bathing, nor fragrant sandal-paste, nor flowers, nor beautifully dressed hair. Only refined, well-cultivated speech — spoken with care — is the ornament that truly adorns a person. All other ornaments wear away and fade with time, but the ornament of good speech lasts forever.
२०
विद्या नाम नरस्य रूपमधिकं... Knowledge is a person's true wealth and beauty 📚
▼
📖 Sanskrit Text & Transliteration
विद्या नाम नरस्य रूपमधिकं प्रच्छन्नगुप्तं धनं विद्या भोगकरी यशस्सुखकरी विद्या गुरूणां गुरुः । विद्या बन्धुजनो विदेशगमने विद्या परा देवता विद्या राजसु पूजिता न तु धनं विद्याविहीनः पशुः ॥ २०॥
vidyā nāma narasya rūpamadhikaṃ pracchannaguptaṃ dhanaṃ vidyā bhogakarī yaśassukhakarī vidyā gurūṇāṃ guruḥ | vidyā bandhujano videśagamane vidyā parā devatā vidyā rājasu pūjitā na tu dhanaṃ vidyāvihīnaḥ paśuḥ || 20||

💡 Overall Meaning
Knowledge is truly a person's finest beauty, and a hidden treasure that can never be stolen. Knowledge brings prosperity, fame, and happiness; knowledge is the greatest teacher, the teacher of teachers. Knowledge is one's true companion when far from home in a foreign land, and knowledge is like a supreme deity to be revered. It is knowledge — not wealth — that is honoured in royal courts; a person without knowledge is no better than an animal.
५५
लोभश्चेदगुणेन किं... If there is greed, what need of other faults? 🪷
▼
📖 Sanskrit Text & Transliteration
लोभश्चेदगुणेन किं पिशुनता यद्यस्ति किं पातकैः सत्यं चेत्तपसा च किं शुचि मनो यद्यस्ति तीर्थेन किम् । सौजन्यं यदि किं गुणैः सुमहिमा यद्यस्ति किं मण्डनैः सद्विद्या यदि किं धनैरपयशो यद्यस्ति किं मृत्युना ॥ ५५॥
lobhaścedaguṇena kiṃ piśunatā yadyasti kiṃ pātakaiḥ satyaṃ cettapasā ca kiṃ śuci mano yadyasti tīrthena kim | saujanyaṃ yadi kiṃ guṇaiḥ sumahimā yadyasti kiṃ maṇḍanaiḥ sadvidyā yadi kiṃ dhanairapayaśo yadyasti kiṃ mṛtyunā || 55||
🧩 Word-by-Word Meaning

अपयशः यदि अस्ति
apayaśaḥ yadi asti

किं मृत्युना
kiṃ mṛtyunā
what need of death
💡 Overall Meaning
If a person has greed, no other fault is needed — greed alone is enough. If a person indulges in backbiting, no other sin is needed — that alone is enough. If one is truthful, penance is not needed; truth itself is the highest austerity. If the mind is pure, there is no need to visit sacred pilgrim places; a pure mind is itself the true pilgrimage. If a person is good-natured and kind, no other virtue is needed — good nature contains them all. If a person has true greatness, no ornaments are needed to prove it. If a person has good knowledge, no wealth is needed — knowledge itself is the greatest wealth. And if a person lives in disgrace or dishonour, death is hardly needed to bring them low — a life without honour is already like death.
