      WHY DID IT CHANGE?

      Why Would Anyone Change a Tool That Worked?

      Hand-axes had worked for almost a million years! So why switch? Historians have a few ideas — tap each one.

      Thousands of generations of practice. Each toolmaker learned from the last and found small ways to save stone and effort. A prepared core gives many sharp flakes from one stone — less waste!
      The climate kept swinging between wet and dry. When rivers and lakes dried up, people had to move more — and lighter, smaller tools are much easier to carry than heavy hand-axes.
      The same change happened in Africa, Europe and West Asia too. Did the idea travel with people moving from place to place? Or did different groups figure it out on their own? Historians are still debating — maybe a bit of both!
      TANTU'S CHECKPOINT

      What Changed? What Stayed the Same?

      Historians always ask two questions when they compare two times: what changed, and what stayed the same? Tap your answer for each one.

      They lived as hunter-gatherers, with no farming

      Hunting and gathering continued — farming is still hundreds of thousands of years away!

      They used stone to make their main tools

      Stone was still the number-one material.

      The size and weight of their tools

      Big heavy hand-axes gave way to smaller, lighter flake tools.

      The way a tool was made

      From shaping one big rock → to preparing a core and striking off ready-made flakes.

      Where people could live

      Lighter tools and better planning helped people live in drier places like the Thar.

      Children learned skills by watching grown-ups

      Learning by watching and copying stayed the way knowledge travelled — for a very, very long time.
