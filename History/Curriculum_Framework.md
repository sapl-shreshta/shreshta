# Indian History Curriculum Framework
_Reference document for planning History chapters on this site. Not a student-facing page._

## Purpose

Turn "Indian history" from a list of kings, dates, and battles into an understanding of how Indian
civilisation evolved — politically, socially, spiritually, intellectually, scientifically,
artistically, and in its relationships with the wider world.

Not every chapter needs equal weight in every lens below — e.g. Prehistoric India leans on
"how did people live" / "what did they invent" / "where", and barely touches "who ruled" /
"what did they write". That's honest to the material; forcing equal length per lens would pad
weak sections.

## The 8 Lenses (apply to every chapter)

| Lens | Content type |
|---|---|
| 👑 Who ruled? | Dynasties, kings, administration, army, taxation |
| 🗺️ Where? | Extent, capitals, trade routes, geography's role |
| 👨‍👩‍👧 How did people live? | Homes, food, clothing, farming/trade, caste/class, women's lives, cities vs villages |
| 🛕 What did they believe? | Religion, philosophy, ritual, temples/mosques/monasteries as belief-markers |
| 📚 What did they think/write? | Language evolution, literature, epics, education, historiography |
| 🔬 What did they invent/discover? | Math, astronomy, metallurgy, medicine, engineering |
| 🎨 What did they create? | Architecture, sculpture, painting, music, dance |
| 🌏 Who did they interact with? | Trade, war, diplomacy, migration with the outside world |

## The Continuity Thread

Rather than a standalone chapter, continuity is a running theme attached mainly to the
👨‍👩‍👧 📚 🛕 🔬 🎨 lenses (the "soft" institutions that persist across regime change).
After every 4–5 chapters, pause and ask: **what changed, what survived, what was absorbed,
what was reinvented?**

Recurring threads worth tracking explicitly across the whole series:
- Village/panchayat governance
- Sanskrit-derived languages and their descendants
- Joint family structure
- Temple/mosque patronage patterns
- Trade guilds → modern business communities
- Mathematical/astronomical knowledge transmission
- Cotton growing and weaving (literally traceable from Neolithic Mehrgarh to today)

## Format Decision (per site convention, decided 2026-09-05)

Unlike the Mathematics chapters (interactive drill + scored quiz), History chapters are written
as an **interactive story** — narrative-first, with click-to-reveal scenes and hotspots rather
than scored quizzes. A recurring mascot, **Tantu the Thread-Weaver** (तंतु = "thread"), narrates
across all chapters and embodies the continuity thread as a literal visual motif (a thread/tapestry
that runs through the page and, conceptually, through the whole series).

Chapter 1 (Prehistoric India) is drafted at
`History/Chapter_01_Prehistoric_India/index.html` following this format.
