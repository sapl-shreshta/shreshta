🌏 The Global South 🌱 BASIC

The Global South means the world's developing and emerging countries, mostly in Asia, Africa, Latin America and parts of the Middle East. Many of them were once colonies.

🧭 Careful — it's not about the compass!

Australia and New Zealand are in the southern half of the globe but are not "Global South" (they're rich). India and China are in the northern half but are Global South. It's about history and economics, not the equator.

The 2026 Declaration said BRICS will keep speaking up for the concerns and priorities of the Global South.

🚀 ADVANCED: BRICS's core argument in one line

"Economic power has shifted, but world institutions haven't caught up."

Developing countries now produce a big share of the world's growth, but the rule-making clubs and voting systems were designed decades ago, mostly by Western countries.

🧲 A multipolar world 🚀 ADVANCED

Leaders at the summit talked about moving towards a multipolar world. What does "polar" mean here? A pole is a centre of power.

⚖️

Bipolar (1947–1991)

Two superpowers — the USA and the Soviet Union — during the Cold War. Most countries had to pick a side. (India led the Non-Aligned Movement to avoid picking!)

☝️

Unipolar (1991–2000s)

After the Soviet Union broke up, the USA was the single superpower.

🌐

Multipolar (emerging)

Many centres of power: USA, China, EU, India, Russia, and regional powers like Brazil, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia.

🧠 Why does India like the idea of a multipolar world?

In a world with many powers, India doesn't have to depend on any single one. It can work with the USA on some things, with Russia on others, and with Europe or the Gulf on others — keeping its freedom to choose. India calls this "strategic autonomy".

🏛️ Reforming the UN Security Council 🌱 BASIC

The United Nations has 193 member countries. Its most powerful body is the Security Council, which decides on war and peace questions.

The "P5" (permanent five): 🇺🇸 USA · 🇬🇧 UK · 🇫🇷 France · 🇷🇺 Russia · 🇨🇳 China — the winners of World War II. Any one of them can veto (block) a decision.

🧠 Spot the problem

Which continents have no permanent seat? 👉 Africa and Latin America have none. India, the world's most populous country, has none. The P5 still reflect the world of 1945.

What BRICS said in 2026

  • Reform the UN and its Security Council to give more voice to Asia, Africa and Latin America.
  • China and Russia repeated their support for the "aspirations of Brazil and India to play a greater role" in the UN, including the Security Council.
  • Backed the Ezulwini Consensus and Sirte Declaration — Africa's joint position asking for 2 permanent seats (with veto) and more elected seats for Africa.
  • Proposed a 10-point roadmap for global governance reform built on three R's: Representation, Responsiveness and Rule-making.
🚀 ADVANCED: Read the words carefully!

Notice China and Russia supported India's "greater role" — they did not say "a permanent seat". Diplomats choose every word carefully. China in particular has never clearly backed India for a permanent seat. Reading between the lines is a key skill in geopolitics! 🔍

India has been an elected (non-permanent) member 8 times, most recently in 2021–22. It campaigns for permanent membership together with Brazil, Germany and Japan — a group called the G4.

🔥 Middle East & Iran 🚀 ADVANCED

The summit happened during a war in the Middle East involving Iran in 2026. This was very tricky for BRICS because Iran is a member — and so is the UAE, a Gulf neighbour with very different views on the conflict.

What the Declaration said

  • "Deep concern over the continued escalation of tensions."
  • Called for "maximum restraint", diplomacy and protection of civilians.
  • Concern about attacks on civilian infrastructure and on nuclear facilities under IAEA safeguards (the IAEA is the UN's nuclear watchdog that inspects nuclear sites).
  • Did not name any country — not the USA, Israel, Iran or the Gulf states.
🧠 PRO: Compare 2025 and 2026

In 2025, the BRICS statement called strikes on Iran "a blatant breach of international law". In 2026, the language became much softer.

Why? (1) The UAE, a member, saw the conflict very differently from Iran; (2) India has close ties with Israel, the USA and the Gulf countries; (3) consensus rules mean the text must be acceptable to everyone. Softer words were the price of getting a Declaration at all.

Is vague language a failure or a success?

Both views exist! Critics say vague words show BRICS can't take a real stand. Supporters say getting rivals like Iran and the UAE to sign the same document — and even meet each other at the summit — is itself a step towards peace. What do you think?

🕊️ Gaza & Palestine 🌱 BASIC

The Declaration had strong language on Gaza and Palestine:

  • Called for an end to the war in Gaza and for humanitarian access (letting food, water and medicine reach people).
  • Protection of civilians.
  • Firm opposition to forcing Palestinians out of their land (forced displacement).
  • Support for a two-state solution — an independent Palestine living alongside Israel — based on the 1967 borders.
  • Support for full UN membership for Palestine (today it is an "observer state").
🚀 ADVANCED: Why "1967 borders"?

In the 1967 war, Israel took control of the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza. The "1967 borders" means the lines before that war. Most of the world, including India, supports a Palestinian state along roughly those lines, agreed through negotiation.

🇺🇦 Ukraine 🚀 ADVANCED

The Russia–Ukraine war (since 2022) was not named directly. The Declaration only said disputes should be solved through "dialogue, consultation and diplomacy."

But it did criticise unilateral sanctions — which matters to Russia, because Western countries have placed heavy sanctions on it since 2022.

🧠 PRO: Why so quiet?

Russia is a founding member and would block any criticism. Meanwhile India, Brazil and others don't want to take sides. PM Modi's well-known line to President Putin in 2022 — "today's era is not an era of war" — sums up India's view: stop the fighting, talk it out, without joining either camp.

🚨 Terrorism 🌱 BASIC

  • BRICS condemned the Pahalgam terror attack (Jammu & Kashmir, April 2025) and repeated "zero tolerance" for terrorism.
  • Backed the UN Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism (CCIT) — a global treaty India first proposed in 1996, still not adopted because countries can't agree on how to define "terrorism".
  • Created an expert network to trace fugitive offenders — people who commit crimes and run away to another country.
🚀 ADVANCED: Why is defining "terrorism" so hard?

Some countries say a group fighting for independence is a "freedom fighter", while others call the same group "terrorists". Without an agreed definition, a global treaty is stuck. Getting BRICS — including countries with very different views — to back the CCIT is a win for India's long campaign.

🎪 India's balancing act 🧠 PRO

India is a member of many different clubs — some of which don't like each other!

🤝 BRICS

With Russia, China, Iran, Brazil…

🔷 Quad

With the USA, Japan, Australia — partly to balance China.

🏔️ SCO

Shanghai Cooperation Organisation — with Russia, China, Central Asia.

🌍 G20

With both rich and developing countries.

This is called multi-alignment or strategic autonomy: being friends with many, and taking orders from none. 🧘

  • China, Russia and Iran often see BRICS as a counter to the West.
  • India keeps saying BRICS is "not against anyone".
  • That's why the Declaration criticised tariffs and sanctions without naming the USA — India has an important partnership with the USA too.
🎪 Picture a tightrope walker

Lean too far toward one side and you fall. India holds a long balancing pole: on one end, Western partners (USA, Europe, Japan); on the other, BRICS partners (Russia, China, Iran). The 2026 summit was a test of India's balance — and most analysts felt India kept its footing.

❌ What was NOT decided 🌱 BASIC

❌ No common BRICS currency

Only work on local-currency payments and linking payment systems.

❌ Not a military alliance

BRICS remains a cooperation and coordination group.

❌ Did not replace the WTO

It asked for the WTO to be reformed and strengthened.

❌ Did not replace the IMF or World Bank

It asked for reform, while strengthening its own NDB and CRA.

❌ Not a "grow bigger" summit

Unlike 2023–24, the focus was on making the existing 11 members and 10 partners work well together (consolidation), not on a big new expansion.

🔍 Different points of view 🧠 PRO

A good thinker looks at all sides. Here's how different people judged the summit:

😊 Supporters say…

  • Rivals like Iran & UAE signed one document — a diplomatic win.
  • Lots of practical outcomes: Grain Exchange, Risk Lab, DPI Repository, Space Council.
  • Gives the Global South a louder voice.
  • Modi–Xi meeting helped calm India–China ties.

🤨 Critics say…

  • Language on wars was too vague to matter.
  • Many projects are "studies" or "frameworks" — will they really happen?
  • Brazil's president didn't come; Saudi Arabia's status is still unclear.
  • Deep rivalries (India–China, Iran–Gulf) haven't gone away.
⭐ The single most important idea

BRICS 2026 was not about building a brand-new world system overnight. It was about building parallel cooperation — in finance, payments, technology, trade and development — while pushing to reform the existing world institutions so developing countries get a fairer say.
