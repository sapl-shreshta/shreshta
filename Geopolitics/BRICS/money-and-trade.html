💵 The mighty US dollar 🌱 BASIC

Every country has its own money: India has the rupee (₹), Brazil the real (R$), China the yuan (¥), Russia the rouble (₽), South Africa the rand (R).

But when countries trade with each other, most deals are priced and paid in US dollars ($) — even if no American is involved! Oil, for example, is mostly priced in dollars.

💡 Why the dollar?

After World War II, the USA was the world's strongest economy, and a 1944 meeting at Bretton Woods (USA) built the world's money system around the dollar. Today the dollar is trusted, easy to buy and sell, and accepted everywhere — so everyone keeps using it. This is called being the "reserve currency".

So what's the problem?

  • 🔁 Extra conversions = extra fees. Two countries must both convert to dollars and back.
  • 📉 Dollar swings. If the dollar suddenly gets stronger, it becomes more expensive for India to buy oil.
  • 🚫 Sanctions. Since most dollar payments pass through US-linked banks, the USA can block a country from using them.
🧠 PRO: The sanctions story

In 2022, after Russia attacked Ukraine, Western countries froze a large part of Russia's savings held abroad and cut many Russian banks from international payment systems. Many countries — not just Russia — noticed this and began asking: "What if that happened to us?" This is one big reason BRICS is exploring other ways to pay. The 2026 Declaration called for removing sanctions that are not approved by the UN Security Council.

🔄 Paying in local currencies 🌱 BASIC

Imagine an Indian company sells rice to a Brazilian company.

😓 The old way (through dollars)

🇧🇷Brazilian buyer has reais
➜
💵Converts reais → dollars (fee!)
➜
🏦Dollars travel through US-linked banks
➜
🇮🇳Converts dollars → rupees (fee again!)

😃 The BRICS idea (local currency settlement)

🇧🇷Brazilian buyer has reais
➜
🔁Converted directly reais → rupees
➜
🇮🇳Indian seller gets rupees

Fewer steps → lower costs → faster payments → less dependence on one currency.

What the 2026 summit did

  • Set up a BRICS Payment Task Force to make local-currency trade payments actually work.
  • Asked for interoperability — making different countries' payment systems able to "talk" to each other.
  • Said clearly there is "no one-size-fits-all approach" — each country can choose what works for it.
📱 Interoperability — example you know

With UPI you can pay from a PhonePe app to someone using Google Pay, even though they're different apps. That's interoperability! BRICS wants something similar between countries.

🚫 Why is there no BRICS currency? 🚀 ADVANCED

❌ No common BRICS currency was launched in 2026.

Many social media posts claim BRICS is launching a new currency to replace the dollar. That is not what happened.

A shared currency is really hard. Look at the euro 💶, used by around 20 European countries:

  • They have one central bank (the European Central Bank) that sets interest rates for everyone.
  • Their economies are closely connected, with shared rules and open borders.
  • It took them decades of planning.

BRICS countries are very different — rich oil states, poorer farming nations, huge factory economies. One interest rate could never suit all of them.

🧠 Why would India, in particular, be cautious about a common BRICS currency?

Because China's economy is the biggest in BRICS. Any shared currency or payment system would likely be dominated by China's yuan. India has a border dispute and a trade imbalance with China, so it does not want to depend on China's money. India prefers using the rupee more internationally instead.

🏦 The New Development Bank (NDB) 🌱 BASIC

The NDB is BRICS's own bank. It gives loans for big projects in developing countries — metro rails, roads, clean water, solar parks, bridges.

📍

Headquarters

Shanghai, China

✍️

Agreed

2014 (Fortaleza summit, Brazil); started working in 2015–16

🇮🇳

First president

K. V. Kamath from India

⚖️

Equal shares

The 5 founders put in equal money, so each has an equal vote — even China!

What the 2026 summit said about the NDB

  • Use it to mobilise development finance for infrastructure and sustainable development.
  • Lend more in local currencies (so borrowers don't have to repay in dollars).
  • Support more countries joining the bank (Bangladesh, the UAE, Egypt and Algeria are among those already in).
🚀 ADVANCED: Why borrowing in your own currency is safer

Say an Indian city borrows $1 million for a metro when $1 = ₹80. It owes ₹8 crore. If the rupee weakens to $1 = ₹90, the same loan now costs ₹9 crore — ₹1 crore more, without borrowing a single extra rupee! 😱 Borrowing in rupees removes this currency risk.

🧠 Remember: BRICS = political & economic club · NDB = the club's bank

🛟 The emergency fund: CRA 🚀 ADVANCED

The Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA) was created in 2014 with $100 billion. It's a safety net: if a member suddenly runs short of foreign money (a "balance-of-payments crisis"), the others can lend it dollars quickly.

🧒 Like friends with a shared piggy bank

Five friends promise: "If any of us loses our lunch money, the others will lend some until you're okay." Nobody hands over the money in advance — it's a promise to help when needed. That's what "contingent" means: only if something happens.

In 2026: BRICS agreed amendments to make the CRA respond faster, and did a 9th "test run" — like a fire drill 🚒, practising the steps so it works in a real emergency.

💡 Balance of payments — quick meaning

A country needs foreign currency (mostly dollars) to pay for imports like oil. If it spends far more than it earns from exports, it can run out — like spending more pocket money than you get.

🏛️ Fixing the IMF & World Bank 🚀 ADVANCED

The IMF (International Monetary Fund — helps countries in money crises) and the World Bank (lends for development) were both created at Bretton Woods in 1944. The world has changed a LOT since then!

In 1944…Today…
India was not yet independentIndia is one of the world's biggest economies
Most of Africa was ruled by colonial powers54 independent African countries
China was poor and at warChina is the world's 2nd-largest economy

But voting power in the IMF (called "quotas") still gives the USA and Europe a large share. BRICS wants quotas updated so they match today's economies.

🧠 PRO: The 85% rule

The biggest IMF decisions need 85% of the votes. The USA has about 16–17% of the votes. So the USA alone can block major changes — a kind of veto. Can you see why reform is so difficult? The country that would lose power must agree to lose it! 🤯

The 2026 Declaration also stressed the importance of the G20 — so BRICS wants to reform these institutions, not replace them.

🚢 Tariffs & the WTO 🌱 BASIC

A tariff is a tax on goods coming into a country (imports).

🧮 Example

A toy made in India costs $10. If the USA puts a 50% tariff on it, American shops pay $10 + $5 = $15. Now the Indian toy is more expensive, so fewer Americans buy it — and the Indian toy maker sells less.

Non-tariff barriers are other obstacles: extra paperwork, strict rules, bans, or long checks at ports.

What BRICS said in 2026

  • Opposed "unilateral tariff and non-tariff measures" (one country acting alone to punish others) — without naming any country, although several members faced high US tariffs in 2025–26.
  • Support a rules-based, fair multilateral trading system with the WTO at its centre.
  • Called for restoring the WTO's dispute-settlement system (the "court" for trade fights).
  • Supported Ethiopia and Iran joining the WTO.
🚀 ADVANCED: The WTO's broken "court"

The World Trade Organization (set up 1995, based in Geneva) has a system to settle trade fights. Its top "appeals court" stopped working in 2019 because the USA blocked the appointment of new judges. Without enough judges, countries can simply appeal and the case goes nowhere. BRICS wants it fixed.

🌍 Carbon border taxes (CBAM)

Some regions, especially the European Union, are adding a charge on imports like steel, cement and aluminium based on how much carbon pollution was made producing them. The idea is to fight climate change.

BRICS worries these are unfair trade barriers in disguise: developing countries are still building their industries, and rich countries polluted for 200 years while getting rich. BRICS opposed such measures when they are discriminatory or protectionist.

Debate: Is a carbon border tax fair?

For: The planet needs less pollution; without it, factories just move to countries with weaker rules.
Against: It punishes poorer countries for a problem mostly caused by rich countries' past pollution, and protects rich countries' own industries.
What do you think — could there be a middle path? (Hint: helping poorer countries pay for clean technology.)

🧾 Small businesses & invoice maths 🚀 ADVANCED

MSMEs = Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises — small factories, workshops, local brands. In India they employ crores of people. Their biggest problem? Getting affordable money (credit) on time.

The Jaipur Consensus: invoice discounting

BRICS agreed (in the "Jaipur Consensus") to work on an MSME invoice-discounting mechanism. Here's how it works, with real maths:

🧮 Worked example

A small Indian company sells handmade bags to a buyer in Brazil. It sends an invoice (a bill) for ₹10,00,000. The buyer will pay in 90 days. But the company needs cash now to pay workers! 😟

A bank offers to pay the invoice today, charging 12% per year.

Fee = ₹10,00,000 × 12% × (90 ÷ 365)
    = ₹10,00,000 × 0.12 × 0.2466
    ≈ ₹29,589

The company gets about ₹9,70,411 today 💰. After 90 days the Brazilian buyer pays the bank ₹10,00,000.

🧮 Try it: What if the rate were 9% and the wait 60 days?

Fee = ₹10,00,000 × 0.09 × (60 ÷ 365) ≈ ₹14,795 → the company gets about ₹9,85,205. Lower rate + shorter wait = smaller fee. That's why BRICS wants cheaper, faster trade finance for small firms.

More help for small firms & startups

  • BRICS MSME Portal — an online place to find partners and information.
  • BRICS Incubator Network — linking startup incubators across countries.
  • BRICS Startup Innovation Fund — being considered, to invest in innovative startups.
  • Better credit assessment and digital financing for small businesses.

🧪 Risk Lab, tax & customs 🧠 PRO

🛡️

BRICS Risk Lab (GIFT City)

Set up at GIFT City, Gujarat — India's international finance hub — to build a reinsurance system.

Reinsurance = insurance for insurance companies. If a huge cyclone damages thousands of homes, one insurer can't pay everyone, so it shares the risk with a reinsurer.

🧾

Tax Cross-Learning Lab

Tax officials from different countries learn from each other — e.g. how to stop big companies from dodging taxes.

🛃

Customs cooperation

Customs officers check goods at borders. Cooperation means faster checks, less paperwork and catching smugglers.

⛏️ Supply chains & critical minerals 🚀 ADVANCED

A supply chain is every step that makes a product — from digging raw materials to the shop shelf.

⛏️Mine lithium
➜
🏭Refine it
➜
🔋Make batteries
➜
🚗Build electric car
➜
🛒Sell it

Critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths…) are needed for EVs, batteries, solar panels, phones, semiconductors. Whoever controls them has great power in the future economy.

BRICS said: supply chains should be reliable, diversified and sustainable, and countries have sovereign rights over their own natural resources.

🧠 PRO: Climbing the "value chain"

Selling raw cotton earns a little. Selling a designer shirt made from that cotton earns much more. Many developing countries have been stuck selling raw materials while richer countries do the profitable steps. BRICS wants members to move to higher-value steps — refining, manufacturing, designing.

Also: China today does most of the world's refining of rare earths. Even inside BRICS, countries like India want more than one source — that's the "diversified" part!

🌾 Food: the BRICS Grain Exchange 🚀 ADVANCED

BRICS members include some of the world's biggest grain growers (Russia — wheat, Brazil — soybeans & corn, India — rice & wheat) and big grain buyers (Egypt, Iran).

  • 🌾 BRICS Grain Exchange — a marketplace for buying and selling grain among members, targeted to start by 2027, to reduce price volatility (wild ups and downs).
  • 🌱 BRICS AGRIN — cooperation on seeds and germplasm (the genetic material of plants — like a library of plant varieties).
  • 👩‍🌾 Global Forum on Farmers' Rights.
  • 📲 Digital Agriculture Network — sharing farm technology.
💡 Why price swings hurt

In 2022, the war in Ukraine (a major wheat exporter) made world wheat prices jump. Countries like Egypt, which imports lots of wheat to make bread, were badly hit. A steadier market helps families afford food.
