Pronouns

Little words that take the place of nouns — so we don't repeat ourselves! 🔁

🤔 What is a pronoun?

A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun (a person, place, animal or thing). The word it replaces is called its antecedent.
❌ Meera loves painting. Meera paints every Sunday. Meera's paintings are colourful.
✅ Meera loves painting. She paints every Sunday. Her paintings are colourful.

Type 1

Personal Pronouns

Stand in for people or things. They change with the person — 1st (the speaker), 2nd (the listener) and 3rd (someone/something else).

Iyouhesheitwetheymehimherusthem
PersonSubjectObject
1stI, weme, us
2ndyouyou
3rdhe, she, it, theyhim, her, it, them
Examples
  • She is my best friend.
  • We went to the zoo, and the monkeys waved at us!
  • They are playing cricket in the park.
  • Grandpa told me a story, and I loved it.
💡 Tip: Use the subject form before the verb (He runs) and the object form after it (Call him).
🎒
Type 2

Possessive Pronouns

Show ownership — who something belongs to. They stand alone, without a noun after them.

mineyourshishersourstheirs
Examples
  • This blue pencil is mine.
  • Is that lunchbox yours?
  • The prize was hers because she ran the fastest.
  • Our house is small, but theirs has a big garden.
💡 Tip: my, your, our come before a noun (my bag) — they are possessive adjectives. mine, yours, ours stand alone (the bag is mine). Never add an apostrophe: yours, not your's.
🪞
Type 3

Reflexive Pronouns

Used when the action turns back on the doer — the subject and the object are the same. They end in -self or -selves.

myselfyourselfhimselfherselfitselfourselvesyourselvesthemselves
Examples
  • I hurt myself while climbing the tree.
  • The cat is cleaning itself.
  • We enjoyed ourselves at the birthday party.
  • Arjun taught himself to play the flute.
💡 Tip: A reflexive pronoun is needed — if you remove it, the sentence doesn't make sense ("I hurt ___" 🤔).
💪
Type 4

Emphatic Pronouns

Look just like reflexive pronouns, but are used to add emphasis — to stress who exactly did something. Also called intensive pronouns.

I myselfyou yourselfhe himselfshe herselfit itselfwe ourselvesthey themselves
Examples
  • I myself baked this cake!
  • The Principal herself gave away the prizes.
  • They built the treehouse themselves.
  • The king himself came to see the play.
💡 Remove test: Take the word out. If the sentence still makes sense ("The Principal gave away the prizes"), it is emphatic. If it breaks, it is reflexive.
👉
Type 5

Demonstrative Pronouns

Point out a particular person or thing — near or far, one or many.

thisthatthesethose
Near 🤏Far 🔭
Onethisthat
Manythesethose
Examples
  • This is my new bicycle.
  • That was an exciting match!
  • These are the mangoes from our garden.
  • Those are birds, not kites.
💡 Tip: If a noun comes right after (this book, those shoes), the word is a demonstrative adjective, not a pronoun.
❓
Type 6

Interrogative Pronouns

Used to ask questions. The sentence ends with a question mark.

whowhomwhosewhichwhat
Examples
  • Who is knocking at the door? (a person — subject)
  • Whom did you invite to the party? (a person — object)
  • Whose is this umbrella? (owner)
  • Which do you like better — tea or milk? (choice)
  • What is your favourite colour? (thing / idea)
💡 Tip: Who does the action; whom receives it. Trick: if you can answer with him, use whom (him ↔ whom both end in m).
🔗
Type 7

Relative Pronouns

Join two ideas into one sentence and relate back to a noun mentioned earlier. They are not asking a question!

whowhomwhosewhichthat
Examples
  • The girl who won the race is my sister.
  • The doctor whom we met was very kind.
  • This is the boy whose kite flew the highest.
  • My dog, which is brown, loves biscuits.
  • The book that I borrowed is really exciting.
💡 Tip: who / whom → people · which → animals & things · that → people or things · whose → ownership.
🌫️
Type 8

Indefinite Pronouns

Refer to people or things in a general way — not a specific one.

someoneanybodyeveryonenobodynonesomethingallsomeseveralmanyfew
Examples
  • Someone left the lights on.
  • Everyone loves the summer holidays.
  • None of the cookies are left!
  • Many were invited, but only a few came.
  • Is anybody home?
💡 Tip: Words ending in -one, -body, -thing are singular: "Everyone is here", not "Everyone are here".
🤝
Type 9

Reciprocal Pronouns

Show a two-way (mutual) action — each person does the same thing to the other.

each otherone another
Examples
  • Ravi and Sita helped each other with homework.
  • The two friends hugged each other.
  • The team members cheered for one another.
  • All the students in class respect one another.
💡 Tip: Use each other for two people and one another for more than two.
🍬
Type 10

Distributive Pronouns

Refer to people or things one at a time, separately — not as a group.

eacheitherneither
Examples
  • Each of the students has a new book.
  • You may take either of the two pens.
  • Neither of the answers is correct.
  • Each gets two sweets. 🍬🍬
💡 Tip: Always use a singular verb: "Each has", "Neither is". Either = one of two; neither = not this one and not that one.
😲
Type 11

Exclamatory Pronouns

Express strong feelings — surprise, joy, shock or anger. The sentence ends with an exclamation mark (!).

whatwhichwho
Examples
  • What! You finished all your homework already?
  • What a beautiful rainbow!
  • Who would have thought it!
  • What! The school is closed today?
💡 Tip: The same words are interrogative when they ask (What is this?) and exclamatory when they express feeling (What a surprise!). Look at the punctuation: ? vs !
