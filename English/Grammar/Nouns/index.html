Nouns

Naming words — every person, place, animal, thing and idea has a name! 🌈

🤔 What is a noun?

A noun is a naming word. It names a person, a place, an animal, a thing, or even an idea or feeling.
👧 Person — Riya, teacher 🏙️ Place — Hyderabad, park 🐘 Animal — elephant, dog 📘 Thing — book, chair 💭 Idea — happiness, courage
Proper Common Collective Abstract Concrete Countable Uncountable Material Compound Overlaps Gender
👑
Type 1

Proper Nouns

The special name of one particular person, place, organisation, day, month, festival, book, etc.

RiyaIndiaHyderabadMondayJulyDiwalithe GangaISRO
Examples
  • Riya lives in Hyderabad.
  • We celebrate Diwali in October or November.
  • Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
  • My school library has a copy of The Jungle Book.
💡 Tip: A proper noun always begins with a capital letter — wherever it appears in a sentence.
👥
Type 2

Common Nouns

The general name shared by all people, places, animals or things of the same kind.

💡 Tip: Common nouns use a small letter, unless they begin a sentence.

Type 3

Collective Nouns

The name of a group of people, animals or things, thought of as one whole.

teamcrowdherdbunchflockclassfamilyarmy
GroupOf…
a herd ofcows / elephants
a flock ofsheep / birds
a swarm ofbees

Examples
  • Our cricket team won the match.
  • A crowd gathered to watch the parade.
  • A herd of elephants crossed the road.
💡 Tip: When the group acts as one unit, use a singular verb: "The team is practising."
💭
Type 4

Abstract Nouns

The name of an idea, quality, feeling or state — something you can think of or feel, but cannot touch.

honestybeautyhappinesscouragefriendshipchildhoodfreedomanger
From wordAbstract noun
brave (adjective)bravery
happy (adjective)happiness
child (noun)childhood
friend (noun)friendship
think (verb)thought
Examples
  • Honesty is the best policy.
  • The soldier was praised for his courage.
  • Their friendship began in Class 1.
💡 Tip: Many abstract nouns end in -ness, -ship, -hood, -dom, -ty, -ion.
🖐️
Type 5

Concrete Nouns

The name of something you can sense with at least one of your five senses — see 👀, hear 👂, smell 👃, taste 👅 or touch ✋.

tableflowerdogmusicchocolateperfumerain
Examples
  • The flower smells lovely. (see, smell)
  • I can hear music next door. (hear)
  • This chocolate is delicious! (taste)
  • The table feels smooth. (touch)
💡 Concrete or abstract? Ask: "Can I see, hear, smell, taste or touch it?" Yes → concrete (cake). No → abstract (kindness).
🔢
Type 6

Countable Nouns

Things you can count one by one. They have a singular and a plural form.

apple → applesbook → booksstudent → studentschair → chairschild → children
Examples
  • I ate an apple and two bananas.
  • There are thirty students in our class.
  • How many chairs do we need?
💡 Tip: Countable nouns can take a / an and numbers, and use many and few: many books, a few pencils.
💧
Type 7

Uncountable Nouns

Things you cannot normally count one by one. They usually have no plural form.

waterricemilkinformationsandsugaradvicefurniture
To count it, use a unit
a glass of milk
a bag of rice
a piece of advice
two spoons of sugar
Examples
  • Please drink more water.
  • Mum cooked rice for lunch.
  • The website has useful information.
💡 Tip: Use much, little and some: much water, a little milk. Never say "informations" or "furnitures"!
🪵
Type 8

Material Nouns

The name of a substance or material from which other things are made.

goldsilverwoodcottonironclaywoolglass
MaterialThings made from it
goldring, necklace
woodtable, door
cottonshirt, saree
claypot, diya
Examples
  • This ring is made of gold.
  • Cotton keeps us cool in summer; wool keeps us warm.
  • The bridge is made of iron.
💡 Tip: The material (gold) is a material noun; the thing made from it (ring) is a common noun.
🧩
Type 9

Compound Nouns

A noun made by joining two or more words together to make a new meaning.

KindExamples
Closed (one word)toothpaste, classroom, sunflower
Open (two words)swimming pool, post office
Hyphenatedmother-in-law, passer-by
tooth + pasteclass + roomfoot + ballrain + bow
Examples
  • Don't forget to buy toothpaste.
  • Our classroom has a big blackboard.
  • We learnt to swim in the swimming pool.
  • My mother-in-law makes the best pickles. 😄
💡 Tip: For hyphenated nouns, make the main word plural: mothers-in-law, passers-by.

👫 Gender of Nouns

Gender is not a separate type of noun — it is another way to sort nouns: is it male, female, either, or neither?

♂️ Masculine

Male persons or animals

king, boy, father, lion, actor, uncle, prince

♀️ Feminine

Female persons or animals

queen, girl, mother, lioness, actress, aunt, princess

🧑 Common Gender

Can be male or female

teacher, child, doctor, friend, parent, student, cousin

⚪ Neuter

Neither male nor female — non-living things

table, pen, tree, book, house, stone
