LIGHT · TOPIC 4

One ray, or a whole beam?

A single arrow of light is a ray. A crowd of rays travelling together is a beam — and beams come in three distinct shapes depending on how far away their source is.

THE BASICS

Ray vs. beam

➡️ A ray

A single straight line showing the direction light is travelling in. Drawn as one line with an arrowhead.

🎇 A beam

A group of rays travelling together from the same source. Drawn as several lines, all with arrowheads.

ANOTHER IDEA

Point source vs. extended source

A tiny, distant, or pinhole-sized source is treated as a single point source — its rays spread out from one spot. A large glowing source like a candle flame or a tube light is an extended source — really many point sources side by side, all sending out their own rays at once.

A single point source (like a pinhole, or a very distant star) sends out rays from one tiny spot, spreading out in every direction.

BEFORE YOU GO

Three things to remember

➡️

Ray vs. beam

A ray is one line showing light's direction. A beam is a whole group of rays travelling together.

🎇

Three beam shapes

Parallel (distant source), divergent (spreading from a nearby source), and convergent (rays meeting at a point).

🔵

Point vs. extended source

A point source is one tiny spot of light; an extended source is really many point sources glowing side by side.
