THE BIG IDEA

Two kinds of sources, side by side

☀️ Natural sources

Give out light without anyone making or lighting them.

  • ☀️ The Sun — our main source of daylight
  • ✨ Stars — distant suns, very faint from here
  • 🪲 Jugnu (firefly) — glows using its own body chemistry

🔌 Artificial sources

Made or switched on by people, especially useful after dark.

  • 🔥 Fire
  • 💡 Electric bulb
  • 🪔 Electric tube light
  • 🕯️ Burning candle
  • 🔦 Torch

BONUS PUZZLE

Why is a room bright even away from the window?

Sunlight doesn't only travel in one straight beam to your desk. Tiny particles of air scatter it in every direction — called the scattering of light — so even a corner with no direct sunlight still gets some brightness.

☀️
Right by the window
🌑
Far corner, no direct sun

Sunlight enters the room and bounces off countless air molecules and dust particles. That scattered light spreads into every corner — which is why the far corner is dim, glowing blue-grey, not pitch black, even without direct sun.

BEFORE YOU GO

Three things to remember

☀️

Natural vs. artificial

Natural sources (sun, stars, jugnu) exist on their own. Artificial sources (bulbs, candles, torches) are made or lit by people.

🌙

The moon borrows its light

The moon is non-luminous. It "shines" only because sunlight strikes it and reflects toward Earth.

🌤️

Scattering fills in the shadows

Air particles scatter sunlight in every direction, so places without direct sun still get soft, ambient brightness.
