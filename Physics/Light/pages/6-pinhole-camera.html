LIGHT · TOPIC 6

A box, a tiny hole, and an upside-down picture

A pinhole camera is the simplest camera you can build — no lens required. It works purely because light travels in straight lines. Build one virtually below.

HOW IT WORKS

Why the image flips upside down

Every point on the candle — the flame and every point down the wax stick — sends out light in every direction, not just one. Almost all of those rays slam straight into the opaque wall around the pinhole and stop there. Only the one ray from each point that happens to be aimed exactly at the tiny hole gets through.

Every point on the candle — the flame, and every point down the wax below it — scatters light out in all directions. Tap through the three stages above to see what happens to those rays.

THE BIG IDEA

Three characteristics of the image

🎯 Real

The image actually forms on the screen — you could catch it on a physical surface like tracing paper.

🙃 Inverted

The image is always upside down, because rays from the top and bottom of the object cross at the pinhole.

📉 Usually smaller

Since the object is normally farther from the pinhole than the screen is, the image comes out smaller than the object.

THE RULE

BEFORE YOU GO

Three things to remember

🙃

The image is always inverted

Rays from the top and bottom of the object cross at the pinhole, flipping the picture upside down.

📏

Distance controls size

A farther screen or a closer object both make the image bigger, following a simple ratio.

🔦

No lens needed

The pinhole camera works purely because light travels in straight lines — proof that rectilinear propagation is real.
