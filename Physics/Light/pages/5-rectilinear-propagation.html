LIGHT · TOPIC 5

Light only ever travels in a straight line

This one rule — called rectilinear propagation — explains pinhole cameras, shadows, and eclipses. Prove it to yourself with two classic experiments.

ACTIVITY 2

The bendy straw test

Look at a candle flame through a drinking straw. Then bend the straw in the middle and look again. Try both below.

Through a straight straw, light from the candle travels directly down the tube to your eye — the flame is clearly visible.
ACTIVITY 3

The three-cardboard test

Three cards, each with a pinhole at the same height, lined up in a straight line. Slide the middle card and see what happens to the candle's visibility.

All three holes are in a straight line, so light from the candle travels straight through every hole and reaches the eye — the flame is visible.

BEFORE YOU GO

Three things to remember

📏

Light travels in straight lines

This is called the rectilinear propagation of light — light never curves around a corner by itself.

〰️

Bend the path, block the light

A bent straw or a misaligned hole breaks the straight-line path, so no light gets through.
