LIGHT · TOPIC 7

Not every shadow is equally dark

Look closely at a shadow's edge and you'll often find two zones: a pitch-black core and a soft, fuzzy fringe. They're called the umbra and the penumbra.

TRY IT

Build a shadow with a point or extended source

Switch between a tiny point source (like a bare bulb) and a wide extended source (like a glowing candle), then drag the screen to see how the shadow changes.

With a single point source, the shadow has just one zone: a completely dark umbra with sharp, well-defined edges.
THE BIG IDEA

Umbra vs. penumbra

⚫ Umbra

The fully dark part of a shadow, where no light from the source reaches at all. With a point source, the whole shadow is umbra. With an extended source, only the inner core is.

🌗 Penumbra

The partly-lit fringe around the umbra, where some (but not all) of an extended source's light reaches. It only appears when the source has real size — a point source never produces one.

BEFORE YOU GO

Three things to remember

🔵

A point source makes only umbra

Every ray comes from the same spot, so the shadow's edges are sharp and completely dark all the way to the edge.

🕯️

An extended source makes both

Because the source has real size, some parts of the shadow get partial light — that's the penumbra surrounding the umbra.

📺

Distance changes the shadow too

Moving the screen farther from the object makes both the umbra and the penumbra grow larger.
