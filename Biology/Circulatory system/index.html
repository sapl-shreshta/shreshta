🚚 The Big Idea

Why Do We Need a Transport System?

Your body is made of about 37 lakh crore cells (37 trillion!). Every one of them needs a steady supply of food and oxygen, and needs its waste taken away. Most cells are far from the lungs and the gut, so something has to carry things around.

🏙️ Think of a busy city

  • Heart = the pumping station that keeps traffic moving
  • Blood vessels = roads, from highways down to tiny lanes
  • Blood = the delivery trucks
  • Cells = the houses that receive deliveries and put out rubbish

📦 What blood carries

🫁Oxygen
🍞Digested food
💨Carbon dioxide
🚽Wastes (urea)
📨Hormones
🛡️Germ fighters
🔥Heat
💧Water & salts

🧩 The Team

Three Parts, One System

The circulatory system is also called the cardiovascular system (cardio = heart, vascular = vessels).

❤️The HeartA muscular pump that pushes blood all around the body, non-stop, your whole life.
🛣️Blood VesselsThe tubes blood travels in: arteries, veins and tiny capillaries.
🩸BloodThe red liquid that carries everything. An adult has about 5–6 litres of it.
Blood goes round and round in a closed loop: it never leaves the tubes. That's why it's called circulation, from the word circle!

📜 Great Discoveries

How We Found Out

For over 1,000 years people believed Galen (Greek doctor, ~200 CE), who taught that the liver made new blood and the body used it up. Then scientists began to test ideas…

1242Ibn al-NafisArab physician. First to describe blood going from the heart to the lungs and back (pulmonary circulation).
1628William HarveyEnglish doctor. Proved the heart pumps the same blood round and round in a circuit.
1661Marcello MalpighiSaw capillaries under a microscope, the missing link between arteries and veins.
1816René LaennecFrench doctor. Invented the stethoscope, first a rolled-up paper tube!
1900Karl LandsteinerDiscovered the A, B, O blood groups, making transfusions safe. Nobel Prize 1930.
1903Willem EinthovenBuilt the first practical ECG machine to record the heart's electricity.

🩸 Part 1: Blood

What is Blood?

Blood looks like a plain red liquid. But if you spin it fast in a machine (a centrifuge), it separates into layers:

🟡 Plasma (about 55%)

A pale-yellow liquid, mostly water. Food, salts, hormones and wastes are dissolved in it.

⚪ Thin white layer (less than 1%)

White blood cells and platelets.

🔴 Red blood cells (about 45%)

The heaviest part, which is why they sink. They give blood its red colour.

Blood is a fluid connective tissue. It makes up about 7–8% of your body weight.

🏭 The Blood Factory

Where Are Blood Cells Made?

Making blood cells is called haemopoiesis (haemo = blood, poiesis = making). In adults it happens in the soft red bone marrow inside bones.

🦴 Which bones?

  • Flat bones: sternum (breastbone), ribs, skull, hip bones, vertebrae
  • The rounded ends of long bones such as the thigh bone
  • Before birth, the liver and spleen of the baby make blood cells
⚡About 2–3 million new red cells every second!

🌱 One parent cell, many children

All blood cells come from stem cells in the marrow:

🌱Stem cell in red bone marrow
🔴Red blood cells
⚪White blood cells
🟣Megakaryocyte → platelets

When oxygen is low (say, high up in the mountains), the kidneys release a hormone, erythropoietin, that tells the marrow to make more red cells. Iron, vitamin B₁₂ and folic acid are the raw materials.

08
🔴 The Oxygen Carriers

Red Blood Cells (RBCs)

Also called erythrocytes (erythro = red, cyte = cell).

  • Shape: a round disc pressed in on both sides (biconcave). This gives a big surface for picking up oxygen and lets it bend through narrow tubes.
  • No nucleus in mature human RBCs, which leaves more room for haemoglobin.
  • Haemoglobin is a red, iron-containing protein. It grabs oxygen in the lungs and lets go of it in the tissues. It carries some CO₂ too.
  • Number: about 45–55 lakh per mm³ of blood (4.5–5.5 million)
  • Lifespan: about 120 days. Old RBCs are broken down in the spleen and liver.
🥬Too little iron → less haemoglobin → anaemia (tiredness)

🩹 The Repair Crew

Platelets & Blood Clotting

Platelets (also called thrombocytes) are tiny cell pieces with no nucleus. They break off from big marrow cells called megakaryocytes. There are about 1.5–4 lakh per mm³, and each lives about 7–10 days.

What happens when you cut your finger? 🔪

🅰️🅱️ Part 2: Blood Groups

The ABO Blood Groups

Everyone's blood is red, but it isn't all the same! Red blood cells carry special markers on their surface called antigens (A or B). The plasma carries antibodies that attack any antigen the body doesn't recognise. If the wrong blood is given, the antibodies make the red cells clump together (agglutination), which is dangerous.

➕➖ Another Marker

The Rh Factor

The Rh factor (also called the D antigen) is another antigen on red blood cells, first found in rhesus monkeys. If you have it, you are Rh positive (Rh+). If not, you are Rh negative (Rh−). About 95% of Indians are Rh+. Your full blood group combines both, e.g. B+ or O−.

How Rh− blood reacts

  • An Rh− person has no anti-Rh antibodies at first.
  • If they receive Rh+ blood, their body makes anti-Rh antibodies.
  • A second Rh+ transfusion then causes clumping.
  • So O− is the true universal donor and AB+ the true universal recipient.

👶 Erythroblastosis fetalis

1An Rh− mother carries an Rh+ baby. The first baby is usually safe.
2At birth, some of the baby's Rh+ blood enters the mother, and she makes anti-Rh antibodies.
3In a second Rh+ pregnancy, these antibodies cross the placenta and destroy the baby's red cells. This can cause severe anaemia and jaundice.
✓Prevention: the mother gets an anti-D (anti-Rh) injection within 72 hours of delivering an Rh+ baby.

🎁 The Gift of Life

Blood Donation

Blood can't be made in a factory. It can only come from people who donate it. Every few seconds, someone in India needs blood.

✅Who can donate?Healthy people aged 18–65, weighing at least 45 kg, with enough haemoglobin (about 12.5 g/dL or more).
⏱️Quick & safeTakes about 10 minutes and gives about 350–450 mL. A new sterile needle is used each time.
🔄Body refills itPlasma is replaced in 1–2 days and red cells in a few weeks. You can donate again after about 3 months.
👨‍👩‍👧Saves up to 3 livesOne donation is split into red cells, plasma and platelets for different patients.
🏥Who needs it?Accident victims, people having surgery, mothers in childbirth, and patients with thalassaemia or cancer.
📅Special days14 June: World Blood Donor Day (Landsteiner's birthday). 1 October: India's National Voluntary Blood Donation Day.

🛣️ Part 3: Blood Vessels

Three Kinds of Blood Vessels

➡️ ArteriesCarry blood away from the heart, under high pressure. Their thick, stretchy walls bulge with each heartbeat.
⬅️ VeinsBring blood back to the heart, at low pressure. They have valves to stop blood flowing backwards.
🔀 CapillariesTiny tubes that link arteries to veins. Food, oxygen and waste pass through their thin walls.

🔍 Inside the Walls

Three Layers & One-Way Valves

Arteries and veins have three layers

1Outer layer (tunica externa): tough connective tissue that protects the vessel.
2Middle layer (tunica media): smooth muscle and elastic fibres. It is thick in arteries to withstand high pressure.
3Inner layer (endothelium): one smooth layer of cells, so blood glides easily.

Capillaries have only the endothelium. That's why substances can pass through them.

How vein valves work

In your legs, blood must climb up against gravity. Leg muscles squeeze the veins as you walk (the muscle pump), and the valves stop blood sliding back down.

⚠️ Tricky Exam Question

The Rule-Breakers: Pulmonary Vessels

🔑 Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Veins carry blood to the heart.
The name depends on the direction, not on how much oxygen the blood has!

🫁⬅️Pulmonary arteryGoes from the right ventricle → lungs. It is an artery because it leaves the heart, but it carries deoxygenated blood.
🫁➡️Pulmonary veins (4)Go from the lungs → left atrium. They are veins because they enter the heart, but they carry oxygenated blood.
🤰Bonus: before birth, the umbilical vein brings oxygenated blood to the baby, and the umbilical arteries take deoxygenated blood away.

🔀 Where the Magic Happens

Capillaries & Tissue Fluid

1. Fluid squeezed outAt the artery end, blood pressure pushes plasma (without the big proteins) out of the capillaries. This is tissue fluid, and it bathes every cell.
2. Swap by diffusionO₂ and food diffuse into the cells; CO₂ and wastes diffuse out into the blood.
3. Fluid returnsMost of it seeps back in at the vein end. The rest (about 10%) drains into lymph vessels.

❤️ Part 4: The Heart

Meet Your Heart

  • Where? In the middle of the chest, between the two lungs, behind the breastbone (sternum). It is tilted slightly to the left.
  • How big? About the size of your own fist. An adult heart weighs about 250–300 g.
  • Made of? Special cardiac muscle that never gets tired and works without you thinking about it (involuntary).
  • Protected by? The rib cage, and a double-layered bag called the pericardium. The fluid between its layers reduces friction and cushions shocks.
✊Make a fist and squeeze it 70 times a minute. Tiring? Your heart never stops!

The four rooms

  • Two upper chambers, the atria (singular: atrium), receive blood.
  • Two lower chambers, the ventricles, pump blood out.

👑 Feeding the Pump

Coronary Circulation

The heart is full of blood, yet its thick muscle can't take oxygen from the blood inside its chambers. It has its own supply line!

  • The right and left coronary arteries are the first branches of the aorta. They start just above the aortic valve.
  • They wrap around the heart like a crown (corona = crown) and branch into capillaries all through the heart muscle.
  • Used blood collects in cardiac veins, which drain into the coronary sinus and then into the right atrium.

⚠️ When a coronary artery is blocked

Fatty plaque or a clot can block the artery. If the flow is only reduced, the person gets chest pain called angina. If it stops completely, that part of the muscle dies. This is a heart attack (myocardial infarction).

⚡ Part 5: How the Heart Beats

The Heart's Electrical Wiring

The heart makes its own electrical signal, so it is called myogenic (beat starts in the muscle itself). A heart can keep beating for a while even outside the body!

1SA node (sino-atrial node), the pacemaker, in the wall of the right atrium. It fires about 70–75 times a minute. The signal spreads over both atria, and the atria contract.
2AV node (atrio-ventricular node) catches the signal and holds it for about 0.1 s, so the atria finish emptying first.
3Bundle of His carries it down into the septum…
4…which splits into right and left bundle branches running to the bottom tip (apex)…
5…then Purkinje fibres spread it up the ventricle walls, so the ventricles contract from the bottom up, like squeezing toothpaste.

🩺 Listen Closely

"Lubb–Dup": Heart Sounds

The heartbeat you hear is not the muscle squeezing. It is the sound of valves snapping shut.

LUBB
  • The first heart sound
  • AV valves (tricuspid & bicuspid) close
  • At the start of ventricular systole
  • Longer, lower-pitched
DUP
  • The second heart sound
  • Semilunar valves close
  • At the start of ventricular diastole
  • Shorter, sharper

🩺 The stethoscope

In 1816 René Laennec was too shy to press his ear to a patient's chest. He rolled paper into a tube and found the sounds came through louder! Doctors still use stethoscopes today.

🎵 Murmurs

If a valve doesn't close tightly, blood leaks back with a "whooshing" sound called a murmur. Doctors listen for it to spot faulty valves.

📈 Reading the Heart's Electricity

The ECG

An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the heart's electrical signals through sticky pads (electrodes) on the chest, arms and legs. Each heartbeat makes the same three-part pattern:

P waveSignal spreads over the atria → atria contract.
QRS complexSignal spreads through the ventricles → ventricles contract. It is big because the ventricles have the most muscle.
T waveVentricles recover (repolarise) and relax.

Doctors count QRS complexes to find the heart rate, and look for unusual shapes to detect heart attacks and rhythm problems. Willem Einthoven, who built the first ECG machine, won the Nobel Prize in 1924.

🧮 Heart Maths

Cardiac Output

How much blood does each ventricle pump in one minute?

Cardiac output = Stroke volume × Heart rate
🫀Stroke volume: blood pumped per beat (about 70 mL at rest)
⏱️Heart rate: beats per minute (about 72 at rest)
🧾Output is measured in litres per minute

😌 At rest

70 mL × 72 = 5,040 mL
≈ 5 litres per minute

That's roughly all of your blood, every single minute!

🏃 Running hard

110 mL × 170 = 18,700 mL
≈ 18.7 litres per minute

Both the stroke volume and the heart rate go up.

♾️ Part 6: Blood's Route

Double Circulation

Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete round, so it's called double circulation. Watch the blood cell travel!

🫁 Pulmonary circuit (short)

Right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium

🧍 Systemic circuit (long)

Left ventricle → aorta → arteries → capillaries in body → veins → venae cavae → right atrium

👍 Why is it useful?

Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood never mix, and blood is pumped out at high pressure twice. Body cells get plenty of oxygen, which warm-blooded animals like us need for energy.

🗺️ Story Time

Ruby the Red Blood Cell's Big Trip

Follow Ruby on one complete round. At rest the whole trip takes only about 1 minute!

1. 🦶 In the toeRuby has handed out her oxygen. Tired and loaded with CO₂, she climbs up the veins.
2. 🚪 Right atriumShe enters the heart through the big vena cava.
3. ⬇️ Right ventricleThrough the tricuspid valve. Then a squeeze shoots her into the pulmonary artery.
4. 🫁 In the lungsIn tiny capillaries she drops her CO₂ and grabs fresh O₂. Now she's bright red!
5. 🚪 Left atriumBack to the heart through a pulmonary vein.
6. 💪 Left ventricleThrough the bicuspid valve. The strongest squeeze of all launches her into the aorta.
7. 🧠 To the brainArteries branch smaller and smaller until she reaches a capillary and delivers O₂.
8. 🔁 Again!She'll repeat this trip about 1,50,000 times in her 120-day life.
Remember the order: Body → RA → RV → Lungs → LA → LV → Body. Right side to the lungs, left side to the body!

🔗 Link to Digestion

Hepatic Portal Circulation

Most veins carry blood straight back to the heart. A portal vein is special: it starts in capillaries in one organ and ends in capillaries in another organ.

  • The hepatic portal vein carries blood from the stomach and intestines to the liver.
  • This blood is rich in digested food: glucose, amino acids, vitamins (and any harmful substances swallowed).
  • The liver checks it first: it stores extra glucose as glycogen, removes poisons and controls nutrient levels.
  • Blood then leaves through the hepatic vein into the inferior vena cava.
🍽️See also: the Digestive System lesson (absorption in the villi)

🩺 Push on the Pipes

Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries. It is measured with a sphygmomanometer (sphygmo = pulse), using a cuff on the upper arm.

A normal reading for an adult
120/80
mm Hg

read as "120 over 80" (millimetres of mercury)

Systolic (top number, ~120)Pressure when the ventricles contract: the highest pressure.
Diastolic (bottom number, ~80)Pressure when the ventricles relax: the lowest pressure.
⬆️ High BP (hypertension)140/90 or more, again and again. Called the "silent killer" because it causes no pain but strains the heart and arteries.
⬇️ Low BP (hypotension)Below about 90/60. It can cause dizziness or fainting.

🟢 Part 8: The Lymphatic System

Lymph: the Second Circulation

Remember the extra tissue fluid left around the cells? It drains into tiny, closed-ended lymph capillaries. Once inside, it is called lymph. Lymph vessels join into bigger ones and finally empty into large veins near the heart (the subclavian veins), so the fluid returns to the blood.

🛡️ The Defence Network

Lymph Nodes & Lymphoid Organs

🫘Lymph nodesSmall bean-shaped filters along lymph vessels, in clusters in the neck, armpits and groin. They swell and feel tender during an infection, so the body is fighting!
🟣SpleenThe largest lymphoid organ, on the upper left of the abdomen. It filters blood, destroys old RBCs (the "graveyard of RBCs"), stores blood and makes lymphocytes.
🎓ThymusBehind the breastbone. It "trains" T-lymphocytes. It is large in children and shrinks after puberty.
👄Tonsils & adenoidsGuard the entrance of the throat and nose, trapping germs we breathe in or swallow.
🦴Bone marrowMakes all blood cells, including the lymphocytes (B-lymphocytes mature here).
🛣️The routeLymph capillaries → lymph vessels → lymph nodes → thoracic duct (the largest lymph vessel) & right lymphatic duct → veins → heart

🏥 Part 9: Health

When Things Go Wrong

Tap each card to learn about a disorder of the circulatory system.

⬆️Hypertension👆 tap
Blood pressure stays high (140/90 or more). It overworks the heart and damages arteries, kidneys and eyes. Linked to too much salt, stress, obesity and smoking.
🧈Atherosclerosis👆 tap
Fatty plaque (cholesterol) builds up inside arteries, making them narrow and stiff (arteriosclerosis = hardening). Blood flow drops and clots form easily.
😣Angina👆 tap
Chest pain when narrowed coronary arteries can't deliver enough oxygen to the heart muscle, usually during effort. It eases with rest. A warning sign!
💔Heart attack👆 tap
Myocardial infarction. A coronary artery is fully blocked, and part of the heart muscle dies. Signs: crushing chest pain spreading to the arm or jaw, sweating, breathlessness. Call 112 at once!
🪫Heart failure👆 tap
The heart doesn't stop. It just can't pump strongly enough. Fluid backs up, causing breathlessness and swollen ankles.
🧠Stroke👆 tap
A clot blocks, or a vessel bursts in, the brain. Remember FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 112.
😴Anaemia👆 tap
Too few RBCs or too little haemoglobin, often from lack of iron. Causes tiredness and pale skin. Very common in India. Eat leafy greens, jaggery, dates and pulses.
🔬Leukaemia👆 tap
Blood cancer. The bone marrow makes huge numbers of abnormal WBCs that crowd out healthy cells. Treated with chemotherapy or a bone-marrow transplant.

🔧 Fixing Hearts

Pacemakers, Stents & Transplants

🔋Artificial pacemakerA matchbox-sized battery device placed under the skin of the chest, with a thin wire into the heart. If the SA node fails or the heart beats too slowly, it sends tiny electric pulses to keep a steady rhythm.
🎈Angioplasty & stentA thin tube with a tiny balloon is guided to a blocked coronary artery and inflated to open it. A small mesh tube, the stent, is left in place to keep it open.
🛤️Bypass surgerySurgeons take a healthy blood vessel from the leg or chest and attach it to make a detour around the blocked part of a coronary artery.
⚡Defibrillator (AED)Gives the heart an electric shock to reset a dangerous, quivering rhythm. Public AEDs are found in airports and malls, and they talk you through every step.
🫀Heart transplantA damaged heart is replaced with a healthy heart from a donor who has died. Christiaan Barnard did the first in 1967; India's first was in 1994 at AIIMS, Delhi.
🤝Organ donationOne donor can save up to 8 lives by giving organs such as the heart, kidneys and liver. Families can pledge organ donation with their doctors.

🚑 Be a Lifesaver

If Someone Collapses: CPR Awareness

If a person suddenly falls down and is not breathing normally, their heart may have stopped (cardiac arrest). Every minute counts!

👀1. CheckIs it safe to go near? Tap their shoulders and shout, "Are you OK?"
📞2. CallShout for an adult. Dial 112 (India's emergency number) or 108 for an ambulance. Put the phone on speaker.
⚡3. Get an AEDAsk someone to fetch a defibrillator if there is one nearby.
🤲4. CompressA trained person pushes hard and fast in the centre of the chest until help arrives.

🎵 Hands-only CPR facts

  • 100–120 pushes a minute, the beat of the song "Stayin' Alive"
  • About 5 cm deep on an adult, letting the chest rise back each time
  • It keeps blood flowing to the brain until the heart can be restarted

⭐ Your most important job

For kids, the biggest help is to get an adult and call 112 quickly. When you're older, take a proper first-aid and CPR class. Practising on a dummy is the right way to learn, never on a real person!

🤩 Part 10: Wrap-Up

Amazing Heart Facts

Tap a card to reveal the fact!

💓How many beats?👆 tap
About 1,00,000 beats a day, and around 300 crore (3 billion) in a lifetime!
🌍How long are your vessels?👆 tap
Laid end to end, an adult's blood vessels would stretch about 1,00,000 km, roughly 2.5 times around the Earth!
🛢️How much blood is pumped?👆 tap
About 7,000 litres a day, enough to fill a big water tanker!
⏱️How fast is the trip?👆 tap
At rest, blood makes one complete trip around the body in about 1 minute.
🐋Biggest heart?👆 tap
The blue whale's heart weighs about 180 kg, as much as two or three grown-ups!
🐦Fastest heart?👆 tap
A hummingbird's heart can beat over 1,000 times a minute when it flies!
🦒Pressure champion?👆 tap
A giraffe has about twice our blood pressure, to push blood all the way up its long neck to the brain.
🔢How many red cells?👆 tap
About 25 lakh crore (25 trillion) of them. That's over 80% of all the cells in your body!

📝 Revision

Points to Remember

  • The circulatory system = heart + blood vessels + blood.
  • Blood = plasma (55%) + RBCs, WBCs and platelets (45%).
  • RBCs carry O₂, WBCs fight germs, and platelets clot blood.
  • Arteries go away from the heart; veins come back.
  • The heart has 4 chambers. The right side is deoxygenated, the left side oxygenated.
  • O is the universal donor and AB the universal recipient.
  • Double circulation: pulmonary (heart ⇄ lungs) + systemic (heart ⇄ body).
  • The pulmonary artery and pulmonary veins are the exceptions.
  • Lymph returns tissue fluid to the blood and helps fight germs.
  • SA node → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres.
  • Cardiac cycle = 0.8 s: atrial systole 0.1 + ventricular systole 0.3 + joint diastole 0.4.
  • LUBB = AV valves close; DUP = semilunar valves close.
  • Prothrombin → thrombin; fibrinogen → fibrin (needs Ca²⁺ and vitamin K).
  • Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate ≈ 5 L/min.
