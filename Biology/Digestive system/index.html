🍽️ The Big Idea

What is Nutrition?

Humans are heterotrophic — we can't make our own food, so we depend on other organisms. When an organism feeds on solid food and digests it, this mode of nutrition is called holozoic nutrition.

Food has nutrients — carbohydrates, proteins, fats, mineral salts and vitamins — plus water and roughage, which aren't nutrients but are still vital. Food must be broken into simpler forms before our body can use it — that's the job of the digestive system.

🔢 Five Steps, One Journey

The Five Steps of Nutrition

👄1. IngestionTaking food in through the mouth.
⚙️2. DigestionComplex food is converted into a simple form by enzymes.
🩸3. AbsorptionDigested food is absorbed into the bloodstream.
🏗️4. AssimilationAbsorbed food is used by the body for energy, growth & repair — or stored for later.
🚪5. EgestionUndigested, insoluble food is removed from the body as faeces.

your body running!

👄 Stop 1

Mouth & Buccal Cavity

Food is ingested through the mouth into the buccal cavity, where it's chewed (masticated) by the teeth, tongue, jawbone and muscles.

⚙️ Two kinds of digestion start here
1Mechanical digestion: chewing breaks food into smaller pieces
2Chemical digestion: enzymes in saliva start breaking food down
💧 Saliva

Three pairs of salivary glands secrete saliva, which lubricates and softens food into a mass called a bolus for easy swallowing.

🚰An adult makes ~1.7 L of saliva a day!

🦷 Tools for Chewing

Teeth — Two Sets in a Lifetime

👶Milk (deciduous) teeth20 teeth. Appear at 6–7 months; fall off between 6 and 12 years.
🧑Permanent teeth32 teeth. Replace milk teeth and last for the rest of life.

Four types of teeth, each with its own job:

✂️Incisors4 per jaw · broad, sharp edges · cutting & biting
🗡️CaninesPointed · either side of incisors · tearing food
⚙️Premolars4 per jaw · broad with ridged cusps · grinding
🪨Molars6 per jaw · many ridges · crushing & grinding. Last pair = "wisdom teeth" (after age 20)

🔍 Zoom In — Tap a Part!

Structure of a Tooth

Enamel
The white hard layer around the crown. It is the hardest substance in the body, made of crystals of calcium hydroxide phosphate bound by the protein keratin.
Dental caries (tooth decay): bacteria turn leftover food into lactic acid, which softens enamel & dentine, forming plaque. Brushing after every meal removes plaque before acid can do damage!

👅 Stop 2

Tongue & Pharynx

👅 Tongue

A fleshy muscular organ attached to the back of the mouth — it forms the floor of the buccal cavity and has taste buds.

  • Tastes food (taste buds)
  • Mixes saliva with food
  • Helps in swallowing
  • Helps in speaking
🫧 Pharynx

A common passage for both food and air — air passes through from the nose to the throat, while food passes through the lower part of the pharynx to reach the oesophagus.

Why do we cough when eating in a hurry? A flap-like valve controls the opening of the wind pipe. Eating too fast can send food particles toward it — the body coughs to prevent choking!
08
🧵 Stop 3

Oesophagus (food pipe / gullet)

A tube about 25 cm long connecting the pharynx to the stomach. No digestion happens here — food just travels through.

🌊 Peristalsis — the wave that pushes food along

Muscles in the food pipe contract and relax one after another in a wave-like motion, pushing the food bolus down toward the stomach.

1️⃣Muscles contract above the bolus
2️⃣Muscles relax below the bolus
3️⃣Bolus is squeezed downward
Peristalsis works even upside-down — that's why astronauts can swallow in zero gravity! 🚀
09
🎒 Stop 4

The Stomach

A J-shaped, muscular bag on the left side of the abdomen. Food stays here for 3–4 hours, churned with gastric juices into a thick paste called chyme.

🧪 Gastric juice has:
  • HCl (hydrochloric acid): kills germs, stops food from rotting, and activates pepsin
  • Pepsin: converts proteins into simpler compounds (peptones)
  • Rennin: changes milk protein (casein) into insoluble curd

🌀 Stop 5

Small Intestine — Bile & Pancreatic Juice

Partly digested food enters a long coiled tube with three parts — duodenum, jejunum and ileum. The duodenum receives bile from the liver and pancreatic juice from the pancreas.

🫘
Liver & Gallbladder

The liver is the largest gland in the body. It secretes bile, a greenish-yellow fluid that breaks fats into small globules (emulsification) and neutralises acidity, making the mixture alkaline. Also regulates blood sugar. The gallbladder stores this bile until it's needed in the duodenum.

🧫
Pancreas

Secretes pancreatic juice with three enzymes:

  • Amylase: starch → maltose
  • Lipase: fats → fatty acids + glycerol
  • Trypsin: proteins → peptides
💚
Do You Know? The liver performs over 500 different body functions and can regenerate itself — as little as 25% of it can regrow into a whole organ!
11
🌱 Where Food Meets Blood

Villi & Absorption

The lower part of the small intestine is the ileum. It secretes intestinal juice (succus entericus) with enzymes erepsin, maltase, sucrase, lactase and lipase that finish digesting carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

The inner wall has millions of tiny finger-like projections called villi — they increase the surface area for absorption enormously.

🔬A villus wall is just 1 cell thick!
🩸 Two absorption routes
🩸Blood capillaries: absorb glucose, amino acids & water-soluble nutrients
🥛Lacteal (lymph vessel): absorbs fatty acids & glycerol

Digested food passing through the small intestine walls goes straight into our blood — ready to travel anywhere in the body!

🧺 Stop 6

Large Intestine & Egestion

Undigested food that can't be absorbed passes into the large intestine, which has three parts:

🕳️CaecumFirst pouch-like part
🌀ColonAbsorbs most of the remaining water
🚪RectumStores faeces temporarily
💩
The solid faecal matter is thrown out through the anus — this act is called egestion (defecation). The exit of faeces is controlled by the anal sphincter.
15
🏗️ Using What We've Absorbed

Assimilation

Nutrients absorbed from food aren't always used immediately — the body converts and stores them until needed. This is assimilation.

🍬 Glucose

Converted into glycogen, stored in the liver — and back into glucose whenever the body needs energy. Excess glucose is also converted into fat, stored in adipose tissue under the skin, or used for energy.

🧱 Amino acids

Used for protein synthesis — building and repairing the body. Excess amino acids are converted into urea, removed from the blood by the kidneys in urine.

📖 Key Terms

Words Worth Remembering

👄Ingestion — taking food in through the mouth
⚙️Digestion — converting complex food into a simple absorbable form
🩸Absorption — digested food passing into the bloodstream
🏗️Assimilation — absorbed food material utilised by the body
🚪Egestion — removing undigested, insoluble food as faeces

⭐ Big Picture Recap

Points to Remember

  • Nutrition has five steps: ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion.
  • Humans have four types of teeth: incisors (cutting), canines (tearing), premolars & molars (grinding).
  • The digestive system has two parts — the alimentary canal and its associated glands.
  • The alimentary canal: mouth, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum and anus.
  • Associated glands: salivary glands, liver and pancreas.
  • Digestion begins in the mouth and is completed in the small intestine.
  • Water is reabsorbed in the large intestine.
  • Excess glucose is stored as glycogen (liver) and fat (adipose tissue).
