Mixtures & Separation

Chapter 2 · Separation of Substances

Today we become kitchen scientists 👩‍🔬 — we'll learn what mixtures are and all the clever tricks used to pull them apart: picking, blowing, shaking, magnets, heating, filtering, spinning and boiling!

🍵 Mixtures are Everywhere!

Most things we use or eat every day are mixtures.

Your cup of tea ☕ is a mixture of:

🥛 Milk💧 Water🍃 Tea leaves🍬 Sugar

More everyday mixtures: 🌬️ air, 🥗 salad, 🍋 lemonade, 🌊 sea water, 🍪 cookie dough, 🪨 soil, 🥤 soft drinks.

✅ Pure Substance

Made up of only one kind of particle.

Pure substances are either:

  • Elements — e.g. gold, oxygen, iron
  • Compounds — e.g. water (H₂O), common salt

🥣 Mixture

Pure substances usually mix together to form mixtures.

e.g. salt (compound) + water (compound) ➡️ salt solution (mixture)

🥣 What is a Mixture?

A mixture is made up of two or more substances mixed together in ANY ratio, and the substances can be separated by simple physical methods.

🧩 Components (Constituents)

The substances which make up a mixture are called its components or constituents.

In a mixture, the components keep their own properties.

🍬 Sugar in water still tastes sweet. 🧲 Iron in sand is still attracted by a magnet.

🔑 3 Key Features

  • Any ratio: 1 spoon or 3 spoons of sugar in water — both are sugar-water.
  • Properties kept: each part behaves as it did alone.
  • Easily separated: by physical methods like picking, sieving, filtering, evaporating.
MixtureCompound (for comparison)
Parts mixed in any ratioElements joined in a fixed ratio (water is always H₂O)
Parts keep their propertiesNew substance with new properties
Separated by physical methodsNeeds chemical methods to break

🔍 Types of Mixtures

A mixture can be homogeneous or heterogeneous.

🟦 Homogeneous Mixture

Uniform composition throughout — the components cannot be seen or told apart by eyes.

e.g. salt solution, sugar solution, lemonade, air, brass, vinegar.

🟫 Heterogeneous Mixture

Not uniform — the components can be seen by eyes and told apart easily.

e.g. sand and salt, mud and water, oil and water, salad, dal with stones.

💡 Easy test: If you can see different bits, or layers, or things settling — it's heterogeneous. If it looks the same everywhere, even under a strong light — it's homogeneous.

✂️ Why Do We Separate Mixtures?

Sometimes a mixture contains harmful or unwanted (undesirable) substances. We remove them to get pure or useful substances.

🚫 Remove harmful parts

Stones from rice & dal, germs & mud from drinking water.

✅ Get useful parts

Salt from sea water, butter from curd, grain from crops.

🔬 Get pure substances

Pure water for medicines, pure metals from ores.

🧠 Methods of Separation

The method we choose depends on the properties of the components. Different mixtures need different methods!

✋ Separating Solids from Solids: Handpicking

Handpicking is used to remove unwanted components that can be seen by naked eyes. Their size, shape and colour are different from the main component.

🏠 At home: picking stones from rice & dal, removing rotten grains, sorting coloured beads, taking out chillies from a dish.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate a mixture of dal and small stones by handpicking

Materials required: Dal containing small stones, a plate

Procedure: Take some dal in a plate and look for the small pieces of stones or pebbles. Handpick the stones to get pure dal.

Observations: Since the size and colour of small pebbles are different from the rest of the dal, they can be handpicked and removed.

Conclusion: Dal is separated from stone pieces by the handpicking method.

⚠️ Limit: Handpicking works only when the amount is small and the unwanted parts are big enough to see. You can't handpick sand out of sugar!

🌾 Threshing

Threshing is the process of loosening the edible part of cereal grains (like wheat and rice) from the scaly, inedible chaff that surrounds them, and freeing the grains from the stalks.

🤲 By hand

Farmers beat the stalks on the ground or on a hard stone/wooden surface so the grains fall off.

🐂 Using animals

In some villages, bullocks walk over the harvested crop to loosen the grains.

🚜 By machine

A mechanical thresher or a combine harvester is used when a large quantity of grain is involved. A combine harvester cuts the crop and threshes it in one go!

🌬️ Winnowing

Winnowing separates the heavier and lighter components of a mixture by blowing wind or air. Farmers commonly use it to separate light husk particles from heavier seeds of grain.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate a mixture of husk and grains by winnowing

Materials required: Mixture of husk and grains, a winnower (soop / chata) and a stool

Procedure: Take a mixture of grain (wheat) and husk in a winnower. Stand on a stool and let the husk and grain fall from a height by shaking the winnower in the air.

Observations: Wheat, being heavier, falls down to one side, forming a heap. The husk, being lighter, is blown away by the air and forms a separate heap farther away from the wheat.

Grains fall near you, husk falls away from you — because grains are heavier than husk.

Conclusion: Grains are separated from husk by winnowing.

🥣 Sieving

If the components of a mixture have particles of different sizes, they can be separated with a sieve. The size of the pores (holes) in the sieve depends on the size of the particles to be separated.

🏠 Examples: sieving atta at home, separating pebbles and stones from sand at construction sites, sifting flour for baking a cake, a tea strainer.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate wheat flour from husk by sieving

Materials required: Flour containing husk, a sieve with small pores

Procedure: Take a mixture of wheat flour containing husk in a sieve with small pores. Shake the sieve to separate the bigger particles of husk.

Observations: Finer particles of wheat flour pass through the sieve and drop down. The bigger particles of husk are left behind on the sieve.

Conclusion: Wheat flour is separated from husk by sieving.

🧲 Magnetic Separation

Magnetic impurities like iron can be separated from other non-magnetic components using a magnet. Magnetic impurities are attracted towards the magnet, leaving behind the non-magnetic components.

🧲 Magnetic: iron, nickel, cobalt, steel pins  |  🚫 Non-magnetic: sand, sulphur, salt, sawdust, plastic, paper

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate a mixture of sand and all-pins by magnetic separation

Materials required: Sand, all-pins, a bowl, a magnet

Procedure: Take a mixture of sand and all-pins in a bowl and run a magnet over it.

Observations: When the magnet is run over the mixture, the all-pins get attracted to the magnet and sand is left behind in the bowl.

Conclusion: Sand is separated from all-pins by magnetic separation.

🏆 Competition Window: The Magnetic Separator

⚙️ Magnetic separation in industry

During the extraction of a metal from its ore, magnetic separation is used to remove magnetic impurities from the ore.

A magnetic separator has a belt moving over two rollers — one of them is a magnetic roller.

  • Powdered ore is poured onto the moving belt from a hopper.
  • Magnetic material (iron) sticks to the belt and falls close to / along the roller.
  • Non-magnetic impurities are not held, so they get thrown farther away from the roller.
  • Two separate heaps are formed! 🎉

Also used in 🗑️ scrap yards and recycling plants to pull out iron and steel from garbage.

❓ DO YOU KNOW? — Sublimation

Camphor is a solid which changes directly into vapour on heating, without becoming a liquid first. This process is called sublimation.

Camphor can be separated from a mixture of sand and camphor by heating. Camphor changes to vapours, which can be collected on a cool surface (like an inverted funnel), and sand is left behind.

Other substances that sublime: naphthalene (moth balls)ammonium chlorideiodinedry ice (solid CO₂)

💧 Separating Solids from Liquids

When a solid is put in a liquid, it may or may not dissolve in it. Different methods are used for soluble and insoluble solids.

🧂 Soluble solid (dissolves)

e.g. salt in water, sugar in water, copper sulphate in water

Methods:

  • Evaporation
  • Crystallisation

🪨 Insoluble solid (does not dissolve)

e.g. sand in water, mud in water, chalk in water, tea leaves in tea

Methods:

  • Sedimentation & Decantation
  • Loading
  • Filtration
  • Centrifugation

☀️ Evaporation

The conversion of a liquid into vapours is called evaporation. It takes place from open surfaces even at ordinary temperature.

🌡️ The rate of evaporation increases with increase in temperature (also with more surface area and more wind — that's why clothes dry faster on a hot, windy day!).

🌊 Salt is obtained from sea water by evaporation: sea water is trapped in shallow pits, the sun evaporates the water, and salt is left behind.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate salt from a salt solution by evaporation

Materials required: Common salt, water, beaker, stirrer, tripod stand, wire gauze, china dish and burner

Procedure: Make a solution of common salt in a beaker by dissolving salt in water. Pour the solution into a china dish and heat it by keeping it on a wire gauze on a tripod stand.

Observations: Water evaporates slowly and salt is left behind in the china dish.

Conclusion: Water is removed by evaporation and salt is left in the china dish.

💎 Crystallisation

While evaporating a solution, a stage comes when very little solvent is left — this is a concentrated solution. When it is allowed to cool slowly, crystals of the dissolved substance start separating. This process is called crystallisation.

⭐ It is used to obtain a substance in its pure form — e.g. pure sugar crystals, pure salt, alum and copper sulphate crystals.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To get pure crystals from an impure solution

Materials required: Copper sulphate salt, water, beaker, stirrer, china dish, filter paper, wire gauze

Procedure: Dissolve copper sulphate (impure salt) in water by heating it. Filter the solution into a china dish (to remove insoluble dirt) and heat it. When the solution becomes concentrated, switch off the burner. Let it cool slowly and undisturbed. Crystals of the pure substance are formed — dry them.

Observations: When the solution in the china dish is kept undisturbed for some time, blue crystals of pure copper sulphate are obtained.

Conclusion: Pure crystals are separated from an impure solution by crystallisation.

⏳ Sedimentation & Decantation

⬇️ Sedimentation

When a mixture of an insoluble, heavier solid and a liquid is left undisturbed, the heavier solid settles down at the bottom. This is sedimentation. The settled solid is called the sediment.

🫗 Decantation

Decantation is the process of gently pouring out the liquid (called the supernatant) without disturbing the sediment.

🏠 Everyday examples: washing rice or dal (water with dirt is poured off), letting muddy water settle in a bucket, separating tea leaves by pouring tea carefully, clearing river water in a tank.

🧱 Loading — Speeding up Sedimentation

Very fine clay particles in water take a long time to settle. If we add alum (phitkari) to muddy water, the tiny particles stick together, become heavier and settle down quickly.

This process of adding a substance to make fine particles settle faster is called loading.

7Give a few examples in which loading is used before separation of the mixture.

(ii) To remove impurities from drinking water by settling the impurities.

🧻 Filtration

Filtration is used to remove an insoluble solid from a liquid by passing the mixture through a filter (filter paper, fine cloth, strainer, sand bed).

The clear liquid that passes through is the filtrate. The solid left on the filter paper is the residue.

🏠 Filtration around us

  • Tea strainer separates tea leaves from tea ☕
  • Water purifiers & candle filters at home 🚰
  • Making paneer using a muslin cloth 🧀
  • Straining juice to remove pulp & seeds 🍊
  • Vacuum cleaner & AC filters trap dust 🌀

🌀 Centrifugation

Centrifugation separates the lighter and heavier components of a mixture by spinning (rotating) it at a very high speed. Heavier particles are pushed to the bottom, lighter ones stay on top.

The clear liquid on top is the supernatant; the solid collected at the bottom is the residue.

🥛 Cream from milk

Dairies spin milk in a cream separator — the lighter cream separates from the heavier skimmed milk.

🩸 Blood tests

Labs spin blood samples to separate blood cells from the liquid plasma.

👕 Washing machine

The dryer spins clothes fast to throw the water out.

🧈 Butter from curd

Churning curd (a form of spinning) separates butter from buttermilk.

🧪 Solutions: Solute, Solvent & Solubility

A solution is a mixture in which a solid is dissolved in a liquid. The property of a solid dissolving in a liquid is called solubility.

A substance that dissolves is the solute; the liquid it dissolves in is the solvent.

Solute + Solvent = Solution   🍬 + 💧 = 🥤 sugar solution

💧 Water is called the universal solvent because it dissolves so many substances.

🥛 Unsaturated

Has less solute than the maximum that can be dissolved. It can still dissolve more.

🧂 Saturated

Has the maximum amount of solute that can be dissolved at that temperature. Extra solute settles at the bottom.

🔥 Supersaturated

Has more solute than the maximum. Made by heating a saturated solution and dissolving more solute. Since solubility increases with temperature, it holds more solute than a saturated solution.

🫗 Separation of Liquids

🤝 Miscible liquids

Liquids that mix completely with each other and cannot be seen separately.

e.g. milk and water, ink and water, honey and water, alcohol and water.

Separated by: distillation / fractional distillation

🙅 Immiscible liquids

Liquids that do not mix and form separate layers.

e.g. oil and water, kerosene and water, petrol and water, mustard oil and water.

Separated by: separating funnel

❓ DO YOU KNOW? — Emulsion

Oil and water are immiscible, but if a little soap solution or detergent is added and shaken, the mixture gets a milky appearance and becomes stable. This is called an emulsion. 🧼 That's how soap washes oily dirt off your hands! Milk and mayonnaise are also emulsions.

♨️ Distillation

A mixture of two miscible liquids can be separated by distillation — boiling the liquid mixture and condensing the liquid which vaporises first.

Simple distillation works only if the liquids' boiling points differ by more than 25 K (25 °C).

Condensation is the process by which vapours turn back into liquid on cooling — e.g. steam turns into water drops on a cold lid. 🍲

Distillation = Evaporation (boiling) + Condensation

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate a mixture of water and alcohol by distillation

Materials required: Alcohol, water, beaker, condenser, round-bottom (distillation) flask, thermometer, clamp stands, burner

Procedure: Set up the distillation apparatus and take the mixture of water and alcohol in the distillation flask. Connect the condenser's cold-water inlet to the tap. Heat the flask and let the mixture boil. Collect the vapours in a beaker/conical flask after condensing them through the condenser.

Observations: Alcohol boils first since its boiling point is lower (about 78–80 °C). It vaporises and is condensed first. Water, with the higher boiling point (100 °C), remains in the flask.

Conclusion: A mixture of water and alcohol is separated by distillation.

🌍 Real life: Distillation makes distilled water for medicines, injections and car batteries, and gets drinking water from sea water on ships.

🏆 Competition Window: Fractional Distillation

🧪 When boiling points are close together

If the boiling points of two miscible liquids are very close (less than about 25 K apart), simple distillation cannot separate them.

Why? At the boiling point of the more volatile liquid, there are enough vapours of the less volatile liquid too — so both liquids distil together.

Such mixtures are separated by fractional distillation, which involves repeated distillations and condensations.

It uses a fractionating column — a long glass tube with a wide bore, packed with glass beads, small stones or porcelain pieces. The column:

  • increases the cooling surface area, and
  • provides hurdles to the rising vapours and falling liquid,

so only the lower-boiling liquid reaches the top and passes into the condenser.

🛢️ Used to separate crude oil into petrol, diesel, kerosene, etc., and liquid air into oxygen, nitrogen and argon.

🍶 Separating Funnel

To separate a mixture of two immiscible (insoluble) liquids, we use a separating funnel. It is made of glass, has a narrow end and a stopcock (tap) at the bottom to control the flow of liquid.

🧪 ACTIVITY CORNER — To separate a mixture of oil and water by separating funnel

Materials required: Oil, water, beaker, separating funnel, stand

Procedure: Take a mixture of oil and water in a separating funnel, shake it well and leave it undisturbed for some time. Open the stopcock and collect the lower layer.

Observations: Oil and water form two separate layers. Oil, being lighter, floats on water. On opening the stopcock, water (the lower layer) is collected first. Close the stopcock just as the oil reaches it, then take the oil out into a separate beaker.

Conclusion: A mixture of oil and water is separated by a separating funnel.

🗺️ Concept Map — Which Method When?

🪨 Solid – Solid

  • Handpicking — different colour, size, shape
  • Threshing & Winnowing — heavier vs lighter
  • Sieving — different particle size
  • Magnetic separation — magnetic vs non-magnetic (iron filings from sulphur)
  • Sublimation — one part turns directly to vapour

🧂 Solid – Liquid

  • Evaporation — liquid to vapour, e.g. salt from sea water
  • Crystallisation — pure crystals from a solution
  • Sedimentation & Decantation — heavy parts settle; pour off liquid
  • Filtration — removes insoluble solid
  • Centrifugation — spin fast to separate lighter parts

💧 Liquid – Liquid

  • Separating funnel — two immiscible liquids (kerosene & water)
  • Distillation — two miscible liquids with different boiling points
  • Fractional distillation — boiling points very close

🎮 Quiz Time: Pick the Method!

🎉 Let's Recap! 🎉

  • A mixture = two or more substances in any ratio; parts keep their properties and are separated by physical methods.
  • Homogeneous = uniform (salt solution); heterogeneous = parts visible (mud & water).
  • The method depends on the property that differs: size, colour, weight, magnetism, solubility, boiling point.
  • Solids: handpicking, threshing, winnowing, sieving, magnetic separation, sublimation.
  • Solid in liquid: evaporation, crystallisation, sedimentation, decantation, loading, filtration, centrifugation.
  • Liquids: separating funnel (immiscible), distillation & fractional distillation (miscible).
  • Solute + solvent = solution; unsaturated → saturated → supersaturated.
