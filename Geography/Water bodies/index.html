🌍 1. What is a Water Body?

A body of water (or water body) is any significant accumulation of water on the Earth's surface. Earth is often called the Watery Planet because nearly 71% of its surface is covered with water. The term usually refers to oceans, seas and lakes — but rivers, streams and canals, where water moves from one place to another, are also considered bodies of water. The Earth's hydrosphere consists of water on and below the surface of the Earth.

💧 The Water Cycle (Hydrological Cycle)

There is a continuous interchange of water between the oceans, atmosphere and land — this is called the hydrological cycle or water cycle. The differential heating of the Earth by the sun causes water to evaporate; water vapour condenses in the cooler upper atmosphere to form clouds, which fall back as rain, snow or hail (precipitation). This water flows into rivers as runoff, soaks into the ground as recharge, or returns to the oceans as discharge. Because of this circulation, water exists in three states — solid, liquid and gas (snow, water and vapour). Water cycle is the single process through which the three realms of the Earth — lithosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere — are connected.

📖 Glossary

Continent
The large masses of land.
Sea
Expanse of saltwater surrounding the continents.
Lake
A hollow in the Earth's surface filled permanently with water. It may be fresh or brackish (salty).
River
A stream of water flowing from higher ground level to lower ground level. The origin of a river is called the 'source of the river'.
Isthmus
Narrow strip of land with water on each side connecting two masses of land.
Catchment area
An area from which surface runoff is carried away by a single drainage system.

📝 Synopsis

  • The Earth is a watery planet with 71% of its surface being covered with water. All oceans and seas are interconnected. There is a continuous interchange of water between the oceans, atmosphere and land known as the water cycle.
  • The different types of water bodies are oceans, seas, lakes and rivers. There are five important oceans like Pacific Ocean, Atlantic Ocean, Indian Ocean, Arctic Ocean and Southern Ocean.
  • Every ocean has its own characteristics. Pacific Ocean being the largest and Atlantic Ocean being the second largest. The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and the Southern Ocean remains frozen.
  • Oceans regulate climate, support marine life, provide food, it plays a role in the water cycle and is a source of mineral.
  • Seas are marginal or inland.
  • Lakes may be saline or freshwater and supports agriculture, helps in fishing, used for recreation, controls flood, maintains groundwater level etc.
  • Rivers may either be perennial (e.g. Ganga) or non-perennial (e.g. Krishna).
