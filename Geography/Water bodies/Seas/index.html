Seas — Marginal & Inland

Geography Class 6, Ch. 3 — Water Bodies

4. Sea — Definition

"A sea is a large body of saltwater that is surrounded in whole or part by land."

Often the boundaries of a particular sea are defined by peninsular or island chains. Seas, though vast, are smaller and shallower than the ocean. Their waves are smaller, and no big currents flow through them. The sea contains a great variety of salts and minerals in solution — including magnesium chloride, magnesium sulphate, potassium sulphate and calcium carbonate.

(i) Marginal sea: A marginal sea is a partially enclosed body of water that is located between the open ocean and the coastal zone, e.g. the Bering Sea, bordered by Russia, Alaska (USA) and separating the North Pacific Ocean from the Arctic via the Bering Strait, with the Gulf of Alaska and Chukchi Sea nearby.

🦭 Did You Know?

In summer 2025, the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service and USGS conducted a major cruise in the North Bering and Chukchi Seas to survey walrus populations, collect genetic biopsy samples, and assess ecosystem health. This multi-year study will enhance understanding of age structure and population trends.

(ii) Inland sea: Large lakes in continental areas are often termed "inland seas". They are large bodies of saltwater almost entirely surrounded by land, e.g. the Dead Sea in the Jordan Valley — one of the saltiest water bodies on Earth, famous for its salt formations along the shore (Fig. 3.11) and its high salinity of 250 parts per thousand.
