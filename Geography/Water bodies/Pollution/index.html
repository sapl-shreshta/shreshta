7. Causes of Pollution of Water Bodies

Geography Class 6, Ch. 3 — Water Bodies

There are several causes of water pollution in India. The main causes are briefly described below:

🏙️ (a) Urbanisation

Rapid urbanisation in India during recent decades has given rise to a number of environmental problems such as water supply, wastewater generation and its collection, treatment and disposal.

🌾 (b) Agricultural Run-off & Improper Practices

Traces of fertilisers and pesticides become visible in the nearest water bodies at the onset of the monsoons or whenever there are heavy showers. As the point of entry of such agricultural inputs is diffused throughout the river basin, they are termed non-point sources of pollution.

🚰 (c) Withdrawal of Water

Indian rivers, particularly the Himalayan rivers, have plenty of water in their upper course. Irrigation canals whisk away clean water soon after the rivers reach the plains, denying water to flow downstream — this leads to pollution.

🛕 (d) Religious & Social Practices

Religious faith and social practices also add to the pollution of river waters. Carcasses of cattle and other animals are disposed of in the rivers, and dead bodies are cremated on the river banks.

🐄 (e) Agricultural Waste

Agriculture, including commercial livestock and poultry farming, is a source of many organic and inorganic pollutants in surface waters. Pesticides from agricultural run-off, metals and processed chemicals are also major sources of water pollutants. Sewage and fertilisers contain nutrients such as nitrates and phosphates, leading to pollution.

🛢️ (f) Oil Spills

Waste oil from city drains, tankers and leakage from oil wells causes oil to seep into the sea and pollute the water. Few environmental catastrophes are as dramatic as a massive oil spill at sea — the grim, oil-soaked beaches look as if they can never be cleaned up, and birds and sea mammals die by the thousands.

Real Oil Spill Disasters

1989 — Exxon Valdez, Alaska

The infamous Exxon Valdez poured 11 million gallons of crude oil into Alaska's Prince William Sound.

February 2021 — Mediterranean Oil Spill

An ecological disaster along the Mediterranean coast of Israel and Lebanon; tar deposits heavily impacted the beaches in south Lebanon.

25 July 2020 — MV Wakashio, Mauritius

The oil tanker MV Wakashio, carrying nearly 4,000 metric tons of oil, ran aground on a coral reef on the island of Mauritius's southern coast, reported to be a leak in an underwater connection to an oil drilling platform 17.5 miles away.

2 October 2021 — Southern California Coast

An oil spill released thousands of gallons of crude oil into southern California coastal waters. The source was believed to be a leak in an underwater pipeline connected to an offshore oil drilling platform.

25 May 2025 — Kerala Oil Spill (MSC Elsa 3)

The Liberian-flagged vessel MSC Elsa 3 capsized in the Arabian Sea near Kochi due to flooding in one of its compartments. The vessel was carrying 640 containers, including hazardous materials such as calcium carbide and sulphur.

💬 Picture Study — Look at a polluted riverbank/beach

1. What signs indicate that the water body is polluted? (Look for colour, waste materials, dead animals, foam, etc.)
2. What might be the sources of this pollution? (Think about what kinds of human activity could cause this.)
3. How do you think this pollution affects the plants and animals in the water?
4. What are some long-term consequences for humans if such pollution continues?
5. Suggest at least two practical steps local people or authorities can take to improve this water body.

Analytical & Critical Thinking Questions
  • Why are coastal seas more polluted than the open ocean?
  • The Pacific Ocean holds the Great Pacific Garbage Patch. Why is it difficult to clean up, and what long-term solutions might work better than surface cleanup?
  • If "marginal seas" disappear or degrade, what would be the consequences for both biodiversity and human life?

Glossary

Continent
The large masses of land.
Sea
Expanse of saltwater surrounding the continents.
Lake
A hollow in the earth's surface filled permanently with water. It may be fresh or brackish (salty).
River
A stream of water flowing from higher ground level to lower ground level. The origin of a river is called the 'source of the river'.
Isthmus
Narrow strip of land with water on each side connecting two masses of land.
Catchment area
An area from which surface runoff is carried away by a single drainage system.
