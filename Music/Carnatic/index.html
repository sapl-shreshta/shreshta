🎤 Maitreem Bhajata
Ragam 1: Yamunaa Kalyaani — 65 mEcakalyaaNi janya
  Aa: S R2 G3 P M2 P D2 S  |  Av: S D2 P M2 P G3 R2 S

Ragam 2: Kaapi — 22 kharaharapriya janya
  Aa: S R2 M1 P N3 S  |  Av: S N2 D2 N2 P M1 G2 R2 S

Talam: Aadi  |  Composer: H.H. CandrasEkhara Saraswati  |  Language: Sanskrit
Lyrics: maitrIm bhajata, akhila hrit jaitrIm | Atmavad Eva parAnn api pashyata | yudhham tyajata, spardhAm tyajata | tyajata parEShu akrama-AkramaNam || jananI prithivI kAma-dukhArtE | janako dEvah sakala dayALuh | 'dAmyata, datta, dayadhvam' janatA | shrEyO bhUyAt sakala janAnAnAm || shrEyO bhUyAt sakala janAnAnAm || shrEyO bhUyAt sakala janAnAnAm || —————————————————————— Meaning of 'dAmyata, datta, dayadhvam': The poet uses this phrase from the Brihad Aranyaka Upanishad (5.2.2). Long ago, Prajapati (the Creator) had three groups of children — Gods, Men, and Demons. When each group came to him for a final teaching, he gave them all the same single syllable: "da". • The Gods understood it as "dAmyata" — "Control yourselves (self-restraint)" • The Men understood it as "datta" — "Be charitable (give)" • The Demons understood it as "dayadhvam" — "Be merciful (show compassion)" At that moment, a heavenly voice thundered: "da, da, da — dAmyata, datta, dayadhvam!" The three disciplines for all: self-control (damam), charity (dānam), and mercy (dayā). The terms Gods, Men, and Demons can also represent the respectable, the common, and the aggressive people in society — all of whom need to observe all three "da"s. —————————————————————— About this song: Performed by Srimati M S Subbalakshmi, specially arranged at the United Nations. Composed in Sanskrit by the great saint-scholar Shankaracharya of Kanchi kAmakoTi pITha for the occasion. Appealing for friendship that conquers all hearts, for non-violence and tolerance, requesting nations to eschew aggression and unhealthy competition — aspiring for universal peace and showering blessings of prosperity for all mankind.
