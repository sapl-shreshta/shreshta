📦 What Makes a Solid?

A solid (3D shape) is anything that takes up space in three directions. We describe every solid using three simple parts:

🟦 Face 📏 Edge 📍 Vertex

A Face is a flat (or curved) surface. An Edge is the line where two faces meet. A Vertex is the corner point where edges meet (plural: vertices).

🏛️ Prisms vs Pyramids

These two families of solids look a little alike, but there's one big difference: how their side faces come together.

🔷 Prism

A prism has two identical, parallel end-faces (like two matching triangles or squares) connected by rectangles running along its length. Slice it anywhere in the middle (parallel to the ends) and you get the same cross-section every time!

🔺 Pyramid

A pyramid has just one base and a ring of triangular faces that all lean inward and meet at a single point on top — the apex. The higher up you slice it, the smaller the cross-section gets!

🔮 Recap: Every solid has Faces, Edges, and Vertices, and for polyhedrons, F + V − E always equals 2 (Euler's Formula). Prisms have two matching parallel ends; pyramids narrow to a single apex. Curved solids (cone, cylinder, sphere) aren't polyhedrons at all — they have curved surfaces!
