🧱 Building With Algebra Blocks

🧱

Think of algebra like LEGO! A constant is a fixed block that never changes shape — like a plain 5-stud brick. A variable is a block that can change — its value can be anything. An operation (+, −, ×, ÷) is the connector piece that snaps the blocks together.

An algebraic expression is what you get when you combine constants, variables, and operations — for example, 5 + x.

🔎 Arithmetic or Algebraic?

An arithmetic expression uses only constants (numbers) — like 12 + 8. An algebraic expression contains at least one variable — like 3x + 7.

Spot the variable and you've found an algebraic expression! No variable? It's just arithmetic.

🧩 Monomial, Binomial, Trinomial...

Expressions are named by how many terms (parts joined by + or −) they have:

Monomial = 1 term  |  Binomial = 2 terms  |  Trinomial = 3 terms
"Polynomial" is the general name for any expression with 2 or more terms — so binomials and trinomials are both examples of polynomials!

📝 Quick Recap

  • 🧱 An algebraic expression combines constants, variables, and operations (+, −, ×, ÷).
  • 🔎 Arithmetic expressions use only numbers; algebraic expressions have at least one variable.
  • 🧩 By term count: monomial (1 term), binomial (2 terms), trinomial (3 terms) — and polynomial is the general name for 2 or more terms.
  • 🔁 We can translate words ↔ expressions in both directions.
  • 🧮 To evaluate an expression, substitute a number for the variable and compute.
