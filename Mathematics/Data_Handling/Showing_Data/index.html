⭐ Task 3: Draw a Pictograph Level 2

Next, the club voted for the party game. Draw the results as a pictograph using the key ⭐ = 4 votes. Use the buttons to add or remove half a star at a time.

GameVotes
Key: ⭐ = 4 votes
🤔 Think about it: why would a key of ⭐ = 5 be awkward here? Level 3

The votes are 14, 8, 6 and 2. None of them divide neatly by 5, so you would need pieces like ⅘ or ⅖ of a star, which are impossible to read. With ⭐ = 4, every row is a whole or half star. A good key makes the pictures easy to draw and easy to read.

📊 Task 4: Draw a Bar Graph Level 2

Turn your snack frequency table from Task 1 into a bar graph. Drag each bar up or down (or use the buttons). The scale goes up in 2s, so an odd number like 9 ends halfway between two numbered lines.

🤔 Think about it: why count in 2s and not in 1s or 10s? Level 3

The biggest number is 9. Counting in 10s would make every bar tiny and hard to compare. Counting in 1s works, but you need ten numbered lines and the graph gets crowded. Counting in 2s fits the data neatly and stays easy to read. Choosing the scale means picking a step that makes your biggest value fit nicely.

Read the numbers, not the bar heights: 48 and 44. The side axis starts at 40, not 0, so the gap looks much bigger than it is.

📌 Recap: Collect data with a survey, count it with tally marks, and write it in a frequency table. A pictograph uses pictures and a key. A bar graph uses bars and a scale. A double bar graph compares two sets of data side by side. Always read the title, the key and the scale before believing what a graph seems to say.
